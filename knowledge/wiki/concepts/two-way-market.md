# Two-Way Market

## Summary

A **two-way market** is Fraser's term for a market where sectors, groups, and
stocks stop advancing together and begin fighting against one another. In his
2018 example, energy improved while consumer staples deteriorated, making
selection more important than broad-index direction
([Two Way Markets](../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2018-05-two-way-markets.md)).

## Key Points

- Fraser contrasts a broad bull run with a two-way market: where many sector
  and industry themes once rose together, they begin to bifurcate
  ([Two Way Markets](../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2018-05-two-way-markets.md)).
- In this condition, the Wyckoffian asks what is working and what is not,
  rather than assuming the index trend is sufficient
  ([Two Way Markets](../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2018-05-two-way-markets.md)).
- Relative Strength is central to the read. Fraser uses RS to show consumer
  staples losing leadership and energy-related names gaining it
  ([Two Way Markets](../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2018-05-two-way-markets.md)).
- Fraser also contrasts the 2017 "uni-market", where leading stocks, groups,
  and indexes moved in lockstep, with the more common cross-current market
  where themes emerge and submerge
  ([Swing Trading](../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2018-01-swing-trading-case-study.md)).

## Why It Matters For Wyckoff Reading

- A two-way market raises the cost of weak selection. Index direction alone no
  longer identifies the best trade.
- It makes [[sector-group-stock-drilldown]] and [[relative-strength-in-gear]]
  mandatory parts of the workflow.
- It can turn [[leadership-rotation]] into the main edge while the broad tape is
  choppy.
