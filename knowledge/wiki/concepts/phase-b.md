# Phase B — Building the Cause

## Summary

**Phase B** is the **second** structural phase of a Wyckoff range. Its
function: **build the cause** of the eventual trend move
([book p.182](../../../raw/book/pages/page_182.md)). The cause is built
by the operator absorbing the available stock — buying it in
accumulation, selling it in distribution — during the lateral period.

Phase B begins after the [[secondary-test|Phase A ST]] and ends with
the start of the [[phase-c|Phase C]] shake. It is normally **the
longest** of the structural phases — see "Proportional Duration"
below.

The events of Phase B are repeated **Secondary Tests in B (ST in B)**
of both the upper and lower boundaries
([book p.182](../../../raw/book/pages/page_182.md)):

- [[st-as-msos|Upthrust Action (UA)]] — upper-end test in accumulation
- [[upthrust|Upthrust (UT)]] — upper-end test in distribution
- [[st-as-msow|ST as SOW]] — lower-end test in accumulation
- [[st-as-msow|minor Sign of Weakness (mSOW)]] — lower-end test in
  distribution

## Key Points

### What "Building the Cause" Means

The methodology rests on [[cause-and-effect|the law of cause and
effect]]: the effect (trend move) is **proportional to the cause**
(lateral absorption period). During Phase B, the operator absorbs
stock from weak hands without slipping the price — this absorption is
the cause being built.

In accumulation: the operator buys whatever supply is available, but
does so gradually to avoid pushing price up before the position is
filled. Each lateral wave is an opportunity to buy at favorable prices.

In distribution: symmetric. The operator sells gradually to avoid
pushing price down before the short position is filled.

### Phase B Testing — Both Ends

The book's clearest description
([book p.125](../../../raw/book/pages/page_125.md)):

> "Once Phase B has begun, we are going to be awaiting any type of
> test on one of the two extremes of the range. … Occasionally, tests
> will even be performed at both the top and bottom ends of the
> structure."

These tests serve the operator's purpose: probe the range edges to
**verify how much stock remains** at each end. Tests on the upper
edge probe supply; tests on the lower edge probe demand.

The same test action carries **different labels** depending on the
structural read (UA in accumulation, UT in distribution; ST-as-SOW vs
mSOW) but is functionally one event family. See
[[labeling-is-last-step]] on why the labels diverge.

### Proportional Duration

The book gives a structural guideline
([book p.183](../../../raw/book/pages/page_183.md)):

> "In proportional terms we want this Phase to be greater than Phases
> A and C."

When Phase B is **longer** than A or C: typical, supports a strong
subsequent trend.

When Phase B is **shorter** than A or C (rare): denotes urgency by
the operator and **adds strength** to the subsequent trend move. The
operator did not need a long absorption period — either there was
little remaining supply or there was external pressure to position
quickly.

Hypodermic / V-turn cases are the extreme — Phase B compresses to
near-zero, with the entire structure occurring in days or hours rather
than weeks.

### Diagnostic Cues Through Phase B

The two main diagnostic outputs of Phase B
([book p.139](../../../raw/book/pages/page_139.md)):

1. **Phase B testing direction.** A clean UA / mSOS biases the Phase C
   read toward [[spring|spring]] (accumulation). A clean mSOW biases
   toward [[upthrust-after-distribution|UTAD]] (distribution).
2. **Volume profile during Phase B.** Decreasing volume through Phase
   B = absorption is exhausting available stock (foreshadows
   no-shake variant — see [[no-shake-phase-c]]). Stable or
   increasing volume = supply / demand is still present, expect a
   shake.

### When Phase B Ends

The end of Phase B is marked by the **first signal that the Phase C
shake is starting** — usually a probe of one extreme that fails to
revert promptly. The transition is fluid; there is no single bar that
labels itself "end of B".

In retrospect, Phase B ends where the Phase C shake begins, and Phase
C ends where the shake test completes
([book p.184](../../../raw/book/pages/page_184.md)).

## Why It Matters For Wyckoff Reading

- Phase B is where the **structural read** (accumulation vs
  distribution) becomes more confident. Phase A's events do not
  disambiguate; Phase B's tests do.
- The proportional-duration guideline gives the trader an early signal
  about expected trend strength.
- A **shortening** Phase B in an apparent accumulation is a strong
  signal that the structure is genuinely accumulation rather than
  redistribution.
