# Buying Position, Selling Position, Neutral Position

## Summary

The book frames market stance in three explicit categories
([book p.16](../../../raw/book/pages/page_016.md)):

- **Buying position** — when the price is in accumulation or uptrend
- **Selling position** — when the price is in distribution or downtrend
- **Neutral position** — when there is no interest, no campaign has been
  carried out

This framing replaces vague "bullish / bearish" sentiment language with a
classification tied directly to where the market is in its
[[market-cycle]].

## Key Points

### Why The Three-Way Classification Matters

- It is **defensive first**: knowing you are in a buying position prevents
  you from taking short trades against a verified bullish campaign, and
  vice versa ([book p.15](../../../raw/book/pages/page_015.md)).
- It is **conditional on cycle position**, not on the last candle. A red
  bar inside an accumulation range does not put the market into a selling
  position; only a verified distribution structure does.
- It admits "neutral" as a legitimate state. Not every range is a campaign
  (see [[random-vs-purposeful-range]]). When no professional intent is
  evident, the correct stance is to take no position.

### Position vs Direction

Direction (long / short / flat) is a trade decision; position
(buying / selling / neutral) is a market state. The same buying position
can support multiple trade decisions — entering at the Phase-C test,
adding at the Phase-D confirmation, or staying flat because the timing is
wrong. The position label tells you which side of the market it is safe to
attempt; the trade decision is a separate step.

### Neutral As An Active Read

The most common mistake is treating "neutral" as "I don't know yet." The
book's framing is stronger: if there is no persuasive cause-building, the
market is **affirmatively neutral** and the right action is often to ignore
it until cycle stage is clear.

## Why It Matters For Wyckoff Reading

- Pinning the position before reading events keeps labels honest — a
  [[events/spring]] only makes sense inside a buying position; outside of
  one, the same shape can be a continuation low in a downtrend.
- It is the bridge between [[market-cycle]] (where am I?) and trade
  selection (what can I do here?).
