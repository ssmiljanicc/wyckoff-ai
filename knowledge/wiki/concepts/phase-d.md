# Phase D — Trend Within Range

## Summary

**Phase D** is the **fourth** structural phase of a Wyckoff range. Its
function: develop the **trend within the range** — the move from the
Phase C low (in accumulation) up to and through the Creek
([book p.186](../../../raw/book/pages/page_186.md)).

Phase D begins after the **end of the Phase C shake test** and ends
after the **confirmation event** completes.

The events of Phase D are
([book p.186](../../../raw/book/pages/page_186.md)):

- [[sign-of-strength|Sign of Strength (SOS)]] /
  [[jump-across-the-creek|Jump Across the Creek (JAC)]] — the bullish
  break event in accumulation
- [[sign-of-weakness|Sign of Weakness (SOW)]] — the bearish break event
  in distribution
- [[last-point-of-support|Last Point of Support (LPS)]] /
  [[back-up-to-the-edge-of-the-creek|Back Up to the Edge of the Creek
  (BUEC)]] — the confirmation test in accumulation
- [[last-point-of-supply|Last Point of Supply (LPSY)]] /
  [[fall-through-the-ice|Fall Through The Ice (FTI)]] — the
  confirmation test in distribution

## Key Points

### The Three Phase D Scenarios

The book enumerates three possible Phase D outcomes
([book p.187](../../../raw/book/pages/page_187.md)):

1. **Clean break + confirmation.** Price breaks the Creek (or Ice)
   with ease, the confirmation test prints with narrow ranges and
   low volume, the test holds outside the range, Phase E begins.
2. **Failed break at the boundary → internal LPS/LPSY → another
   attempt.** Price reaches the Creek/Ice but encounters too much
   opposition. Either:
   - It **falls back** to an internal [[last-point-of-support|LPS]] /
     [[last-point-of-supply|LPSY]] within the range and runs again.
     The book uses the phrase "taking a run to jump the river".
   - The operator **pays the price** to cross — absorbing the opposing
     orders at worse levels and forcing the break.
3. **Failed break + opposite Phase C shake → structure resolves the
   other way.** The most damaging case: what looked like an
   accumulation Phase D was actually a distribution attempt
   masquerading. The "SOS" failed to hold; a new Phase C shake
   developed at the **opposite** end; the structure resolved in the
   reversed direction. The book explains this happens when "higher
   forces have been absorbing in the opposite direction" via a
   discreet absorption campaign. See [[failed-signal]].

Probabilistically, scenario 1 is most common when the Phase B / C
diagnostics have been clean. Scenario 3 is the highest-cost diagnostic
error.

### The Creek / Ice As Structural Barrier

Phase D is the moment when the **Creek / Ice barrier** becomes
operationally critical. Until Phase D, the boundary is a reference
line. At Phase D, the operator either crosses it (scenarios 1–2) or
the structure inverts (scenario 3).

See [[creek-and-ice]] for the full discussion.

### Confirmation Is What Ends Phase D

Phase D ends when the confirmation event completes
([book p.188](../../../raw/book/pages/page_188.md)):

- The break has happened.
- The test has held outside the range (no re-entry).
- The test prints with narrow ranges and low volume.

The trader's entry — the canonical Wyckoff entry — sits at the BUEC /
FTI / LPS / LPSY of Phase D.

### Phase D Vs Phase E

Phase D = "trend within range" because the move starts inside the
range (from the Phase C low / high) and crosses the boundary. The
move that follows the BUEC / FTI completes Phase D.

Phase E = "trend out of range" — the markup or markdown that follows.
Phase E is no longer a range phenomenon; it is a trend.

### When Phase D Stalls — Internal LPS/LPSY

A common pattern: the first attempt at the Creek/Ice does not break
cleanly. The next move is back into the range to an internal LPS (in
accumulation) or LPSY (in distribution). The trader can:

- Enter on the internal LPS/LPSY (smaller R:R, but inside Phase D and
  before the boundary has been crossed).
- Wait for the next attempt at the boundary.

This is the operator "running back to jump the river" — see the book's
language at [book p.187](../../../raw/book/pages/page_187.md).

## Why It Matters For Wyckoff Reading

- Phase D is where the **canonical Wyckoff entry** lives — at the
  confirmation test, with the structure fully developed.
- Recognizing the three scenarios is critical. Scenario 1 is the
  textbook setup; scenarios 2 and 3 require different responses.
- The transition from Phase D to Phase E is the moment when the
  structure-based read gives way to **trend-based reading** — see
  [[trend-assessment]].
