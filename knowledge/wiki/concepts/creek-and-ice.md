# Creek and Ice

## Summary

**Creek** and **Ice** are the two structural barrier zones of a Wyckoff
range. They are **not** simple horizontal resistance / support lines —
they are **zones** that mark the **last barrier between range and
trend**. Crossing them defines the structural breakout and the
[[concepts/market-cycle|cycle position]] shift.

- **Creek** — the upper boundary of an
  [[structures/accumulation|accumulation]] structure. Named after a
  stream that the operator must "jump across" to start the markup
  ([book p.38](../../raw/book/pages/page_038.md);
  [book p.160](../../raw/book/pages/page_160.md)).
- **Ice** — the lower boundary of a
  [[structures/distribution|distribution]] structure. Named after a
  frozen surface that price must "fall through" to start the markdown
  ([book p.42](../../raw/book/pages/page_042.md);
  [book p.168](../../raw/book/pages/page_168.md)).

The metaphors are visual aids: Creek = stream you walk along until you
find the place to cross; Ice = frozen ground that holds you up until
it cracks.

## Key Points

### What The Creek/Ice Is — A Zone, Not A Line

The Creek (or Ice) is typically a **band of price**, not a single
exact level. It is anchored by:

- The [[automatic-rally]] (or [[automatic-reaction]]) high (or low)
  that defined the initial boundary.
- Subsequent Phase B tests of the same area — UA, mSOS, mSOW — which
  reinforce or extend the band.

A break "through the Creek" therefore allows for some imprecision —
price may briefly exceed the highest interior probe and re-enter
before the structural break is recognized. This zonal character is
why the book frames the boundary as a Creek (with width) rather than a
line.

### The Two Roles Of The Creek/Ice

1. **Defining the range.** As long as price stays inside the
   Creek/Ice band, the structure is a range. The width of the band
   determines how much "noise" the methodology accommodates without
   re-classifying.
2. **The last barrier before the trend.** Per
   [book p.186](../../raw/book/pages/page_186.md):

   > "That zone that establishes the Reaction event (Creek or Ice) is
   > the last barrier that must be overcome to conclude that one side
   > has final control."

   When the Creek/Ice breaks and the confirmation test holds, the
   trend is confirmed.

### How The Methodology Names Moves Around The Creek

The methodology has a complete vocabulary anchored on the Creek/Ice:

- **Jump Across the Creek (JAC)** = [[jump-across-the-creek]] — the
  upward break event. Synonym for [[sign-of-strength]] in the SOS that
  actually breaks the structure.
- **Back Up to the Edge of the Creek (BUEC)** =
  [[back-up-to-the-edge-of-the-creek]] — the test of the broken Creek
  from above (the confirmation event).
- **Fall Through The Ice (FTI)** = [[fall-through-the-ice]] — the
  downward break of the lower boundary in distribution.
- **Last Point of Support (LPS) / Supply (LPSY)** are the
  function/order-flow names for the same events; the Creek/Ice names
  are the geographic version. See [[last-point-of-support]],
  [[last-point-of-supply]].

### Minor Creek / Major Creek

The book sometimes references **minor Creeks** — internal resistance
levels inside the range that get broken by minor SOS moves during
Phase B without breaking the structural Creek
([book p.158](../../raw/book/pages/page_158.md)). The same applies to
minor Ice in distribution.

- **Major Creek / Ice** = the structural boundary.
- **Minor Creek / Ice** = internal resistance/support that does not
  define the range.

Breaking a minor Creek is not a SOS in the Major sense — it is a
[[sign-of-strength|minor SOS]]. The structural meaning depends on
which Creek was broken.

### Crossing Without Volume

The Creek/Ice can be crossed **without a volume spike** when
absorption is complete. See
[[sign-of-strength]] / [[sign-of-weakness]] — the "breakout without
volume" scenario. The narrowness of the Creek/Ice is not what's being
crossed; the operator's confidence (revealed by the price holding
above/below after the cross) is.

### The Creek/Ice And Internal Pullbacks

Per Phase D mechanics
([book p.186](../../raw/book/pages/page_186.md)): when an upward
attempt fails at the Creek with too much resistance, two options are
open:

- **Fall back to an internal LPS** and run again (the structure takes
  another approach to jump the river).
- **Pay the price**: the operator absorbs the remaining supply at a
  worse price to force the cross.

Either way, the Creek/Ice anchors the structural decision.

### Wyckoff's Original Vocabulary

The Creek/Ice metaphors are from Wyckoff's original teachings. They
are pedagogically useful because they make the directionality
inescapable:

- A creek (water flow) suggests something you cross to move forward.
- Ice (frozen surface) suggests something that holds you up until it
  cracks.

Both metaphors imply that **the breach is structural, not tactical**.

## Why It Matters For Wyckoff Reading

- Identifying the **right** Creek/Ice level is foundational. Drawing
  it too tight produces spurious SOS / SOW labels for minor moves.
  Drawing it too wide misses the genuine break.
- The major/minor distinction is what separates a structural SOS from
  an internal mSOS — see [[sign-of-strength]].
- The Creek/Ice vocabulary is interchangeable with SOS/SOW + BUEC/FTI
  + LPS/LPSY in practice. Different writers use different terms for
  the same events.
