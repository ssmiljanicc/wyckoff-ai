# Labeling Is The Last Step

## Summary

In Wyckoff, **labels are assigned after the fact**. Every named event —
PS, SC, AR, ST, UA/UT, spring, UTAD, SOS, SOW, LPS, LPSY — is a
**provisional** designation in real time. The label becomes definitive
only when the subsequent structural action confirms it.

The book repeats this discipline across multiple chapters. The clearest
statements:

> "We must train ourselves to anticipate the possible outcome of the
> event and be prepared to take action on our behalf quickly and
> decisively." — [book p.149](../../../raw/book/pages/page_149.md)

> "In any case … the labels are not important. We have to think in
> functional terms and what really interests us is to know what is
> happening." — [book p.185](../../../raw/book/pages/page_185.md)

## Key Points

### Why Labeling Is Provisional

Every Wyckoff event is named **by its function in the eventual
structure**. But the structure does not exist until enough events have
played out to outline it. Therefore:

- A potential PS only becomes **the** PS when the four Phase A events
  complete (PS → SC → AR → ST)
  ([book p.99](../../../raw/book/pages/page_099.md)).
- A potential SC only becomes the SC when the AR and ST confirm the
  range ([book p.105](../../../raw/book/pages/page_105.md)).
- A potential spring only becomes **the** spring when the SOS that
  breaks the range follows
  ([book p.141](../../../raw/book/pages/page_141.md)).
- A potential SOS only becomes the SOS when the BUEC/LPS confirms it
  ([book p.158](../../../raw/book/pages/page_158.md)) — otherwise it is
  an [[upthrust|Upthrust]].

This is not a special rule for some events — it is the underlying
discipline for **all** of them.

### Why The Discipline Matters

Two opposing failure modes:

- **Over-eager labeling.** Calling an event "the spring" mid-move and
  committing capital on the assumption that the structure has resolved.
  When the next move is a wide-range high-volume retest, the trader
  realizes the "spring" was actually a continuation low and the
  position is wrong.
- **Refusing to label until obvious.** Waiting until the entire
  structure is visible before naming any event. This is safer for
  pattern-matching but misses entries — the methodology is designed to
  give clues **during** the structure, not only after.

The middle path: name events provisionally with low confidence early,
and **revise as the structure develops**.

### Functional Thinking Over Label Adherence

The book argues for thinking in **functional** terms rather than
**event** terms ([book p.125, p.185](../../../raw/book/pages/page_125.md)):

- The function is: "where in the range is this happening, in what
  phase, with what volume signature".
- The label is: "the methodology's name for that combination".

A trader who thinks functionally can handle ambiguous cases where the
canonical label doesn't fit cleanly — internal shakes that don't reach
the structural extremes, tests that show partial confirmation,
two-step springs, etc.

The recurring example: **UA vs UT** is the same action; the label
depends only on which structural read (accumulation vs distribution) is
favored ([book p.127](../../../raw/book/pages/page_127.md)). The function
is identical; the label disambiguates by structural context.

### The "ST in B" Fallback

The book's explicit recommendation when direction is unknown in real
time is to use the **neutral label "ST in B"** rather than committing
to a UA / mSOS or a UT / mSOW
([book p.128](../../../raw/book/pages/page_128.md)). This is the
discipline in action: keep the label maximally informative without
overclaiming.

### Implications For Trading

- **Stop placement** is robust to labeling — the stop sits at the
  structural anchor (below the spring low, above the UTAD high, below
  the BUEC low, above the LPSY high). The structural location is
  observable; the label is interpretation.
- **Confirmation entry** is the practical application — wait for the
  confirmation test before sizing up.
- **Failed signals** are handled by relabeling — what was called
  "spring" becomes "minor low in a downtrend" once the SOS fails to
  appear (see [[failed-signal]]).

### Connection To Action-Test-Confirmation

The action-test-confirmation pattern ([[action-test-confirmation]]) is
the **mechanism** by which a label becomes definitive. The action is
named provisionally; the test (and its confirmation) elevates the
label from provisional to confirmed.

## Why It Matters For Wyckoff Reading

- This is the methodology's response to the temptation to over-pattern.
  Wyckoff events have specific names, but those names are **inferred
  from outcome**, not visible bar shapes.
- A reader who applies labels mid-structure with full confidence will
  produce confident analyses that turn out wrong. The methodology
  expects ambiguity to be tolerated and resolved over time.
- The discipline is what separates pattern-matching from genuine
  Wyckoff reading.
