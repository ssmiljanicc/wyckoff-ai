# P&F Count Segmentation

## Summary

**P&F count segmentation** is Fraser's discipline for breaking a large
[[point-and-figure-counting|P&F count]] into smaller objectives when the whole
range is not clearly valid cause. In the primary article, he says the whole
base is not always [[structures/accumulation]] and asks how much of the range is
actual "intelligent accumulation"
([Segmenting](../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2017-05-segmenting-pnf-counts.md)).

Segmentation is not a replacement for the book's right-to-left count rule. It
is Fraser's applied answer to count quality: identify the part of the range
where the C.O. appears active, count that segment first, then let later market
behavior judge whether the larger segment was also valid cause
([Segmenting](../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2017-05-segmenting-pnf-counts.md)).

## Key Points

### Count Only The Active Cause

Fraser warns that a decline can stop at a [[events/selling-climax|Selling
Climax]] before strong-hand absorption is present, so counting the entire base
can overstate the objective
([Segmenting](../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2017-05-segmenting-pnf-counts.md)).
He uses Secondary Tests as practical segmentation points because they are
visible on both vertical and P&F charts
([Segmenting](../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2017-05-segmenting-pnf-counts.md)).

### Count Conservative Segments First

In Fraser's ALB example, Segment A is counted from the LPS back to the ST
because that is where the footprints of C.O. demand are clearest
([Segmenting](../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2017-05-segmenting-pnf-counts.md)).
Segment B is treated more cautiously because the earlier portion may have been
continuation of the decline rather than accumulation
([Segmenting](../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2017-05-segmenting-pnf-counts.md)).

### Let The Reaction At Target Judge The Larger Count

Fraser watches what happens when the first segment objective is reached. If a
new [[structures/reaccumulation]] forms there and later produces a confirming
count, that behavior supports the larger count; if distribution forms, the
first segment may be all the campaign receives
([Segmenting](../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2017-05-segmenting-pnf-counts.md)).

### Distribution Needs Even More Segmentation

Fraser separately warns that distribution counts can become impractical because
the downside is bounded by zero. His preferred response is to under-count
distribution and count active selling segments rather than the whole formation
([P&F Magic](../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2016-01-point-and-figure-magic.md);
[Distribution Paradox](../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2016-02-the-point-and-figure-distribution-paradox.md)).

### Mega-Counts Still Need Smaller Counts

When Fraser compresses very large P&F structures, he keeps the mega-count in
the background and still looks for smaller, more actionable counts inside it
([Monster Charts](../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2016-01-counting-monster-point--figure-charts.md)).

## Why It Matters For Wyckoff Reading

- It prevents treating every column in a trading range as valid cause.
- It makes P&F objective work conditional on structure quality, not just range
  width.
- It gives a workflow for uncertain bases: count the clearest segment first,
  then reassess as reaccumulation or distribution appears at the objective.
