# Phase A — Stopping the Previous Trend

## Summary

**Phase A** is the **first** structural phase of every Wyckoff range.
Its single function: **stop the previous trend** and return the market
to a state of balance between supply and demand
([book p.180](../../../raw/book/pages/page_180.md)).

Before Phase A, the market is in a trend — controlled by either buyers
(uptrend) or sellers (downtrend). During Phase A the controlling side
loses control; the opposite side begins to assert. By the end of Phase
A, the market is in a [[trading-range|range context]] and the
methodology's range-internal events become applicable.

Phase A consists of **four events**, in order
([book p.180](../../../raw/book/pages/page_180.md)):

1. [[preliminary-support|Preliminary Support / Preliminary Supply (PS / PSY)]]
2. [[selling-climax|Selling Climax / Buying Climax (SC / BC)]]
3. [[automatic-rally|Automatic Rally / Automatic Reaction (AR)]]
4. [[secondary-test|Secondary Test (ST)]]

## Key Points

### The Four-Event Sequence

The book's narrative
([book p.181](../../../raw/book/pages/page_181.md)):

- **PS / PSY** — first signal of large-trader interest. May produce an
  overextended condition but does not stop the trend outright. May
  appear as a chain of attempts rather than a single event.
- **SC / BC** — final stop attempt. May print with climactic volume or
  by exhaustion (see [[selling-climax]] for the Exhaustion variant).
  Establishes one structural extreme of the range.
- **AR** — the first counter-move with conviction. Confirms the
  climax retroactively and produces the first **Change of Character
  (CHoCh)** — the transition from trend to range. Establishes the
  other structural extreme.
- **ST** — tests the climax to confirm abandonment of the prior
  trend's controlling side. Ends Phase A, begins
  [[phase-b|Phase B]].

### What "Stop the Previous Trend" Means

Stopping the trend means the controlling side **stops being aggressive
enough** to drive price. The book repeatedly notes that the stop is
not a "reversal" — it is a transition to equilibrium where neither
side has clear control yet
([book p.180](../../../raw/book/pages/page_180.md)).

Phase B (cause-building) is where the **eventual** controlling side
takes its position. Phase A merely **clears the deck** of the prior
trend's momentum.

### Phase A Does Not Determine Direction

A key implication: **the resolution of the structure is not knowable
at the end of Phase A**. The same four events appear at the start of
accumulation, distribution, reaccumulation, and redistribution
([book p.79](../../../raw/book/pages/page_079.md) — Chapter 11). The
difference between these structures emerges during Phase B onward.

Early diagnostics that **lean** the read:

- A weak / intertwined AR or one with no volume peak suggests the new
  controlling side may not actually be entering (see [[automatic-rally]]
  / [[automatic-reaction]]).
- An ST below the SC low (in apparent accumulation) is a flag for
  potential redistribution.
- An ST above the BC high (in apparent distribution) is a flag for
  potential reaccumulation.

None of these are deterministic — they are inputs to a probabilistic
read.

### Hypodermic / V-Turn Caveat

The book notes that markets sometimes turn in a hypodermic V with no
Phase B, effectively compressing all phases into Phase A
([book p.93](../../../raw/book/pages/page_093.md)). In a V-turn there
may not be a discrete chain of four events — the climax, the AR, and
the ST happen in rapid succession.

This is the rare case. The textbook expectation is a clear four-event
sequence with breathing space between them.

### Climactic vs Exhaustion Stop

Phase A has two characteristic shapes
([book p.108, p.111](../../../raw/book/pages/page_108.md)):

- **Climactic stop** — wide-range, high-volume SC / BC bar; clear and
  obvious in real time.
- **Exhaustion stop** — chain of repeated PS / PSY attempts,
  decreasing volume, "SC" / "BC" prints with normal range and average
  or low volume.

Both are valid Phase A completions; both produce the AR + ST sequence.
The exhaustion variant is structurally subtler and easier to misread
as continuation.

## Why It Matters For Wyckoff Reading

- Phase A is the **earliest structural read** available. Its events
  set up everything that follows.
- Identifying Phase A correctly means the trader stops trading the
  prior trend (the PS / SC discipline) and starts watching for the
  range to develop.
- Phase A on its own does **not** signal a trade entry — Phase C is
  where structural entries become available. Phase A signals
  "stand aside".
