# The Market Cycle

## Summary

The canonical Wyckoff cycle has four stages: **accumulation → uptrend
(markup) → distribution → downtrend (markdown)**
([book p.14–15](../../../raw/book/pages/page_014.md)). Inside an existing
trend, the market can also pause to **reaccumulate** or **redistribute** —
these are continuations, not fresh primary cycles.

The book reduces all market behavior to two basic conditions: **trends**
(bullish or bearish) and **trading ranges** (accumulation when at the start
of a cycle, distribution when at the high)
([book p.14](../../../raw/book/pages/page_014.md)).

## Key Points

### The Four Stages

1. **Accumulation.** Professionals buy all the stock available for sale.
   When they have verified that there is no longer floating supply to
   absorb, they begin the markup phase. The trigger for the breakout is
   that the path of least resistance has been verified
   ([book p.14](../../../raw/book/pages/page_014.md)).
2. **Uptrend (markup).** Aggressive demand exceeds aggressive supply.
   Late-stage participation by less-informed large traders and the general
   public pushes the price higher until both sides see the level as
   interesting — buyers as a place to close, sellers as a place to short
   ([book p.15](../../../raw/book/pages/page_015.md)).
3. **Distribution.** A ceiling forms. Professionals finish distributing
   stock previously bought, supplied by the late greedy buyers
   ([book p.15](../../../raw/book/pages/page_015.md)).
4. **Downtrend (markdown).** When the path of least resistance is verified
   downward, the bearish phase starts. Sellers' aggressive supply now
   exceeds buyers' demand ([book p.15](../../../raw/book/pages/page_015.md)).

### Pauses Inside A Trend

If, after pause, price continues to climb, the structure is a
**reaccumulation**. If after pause it continues to fall, it is a
**redistribution** ([book p.15](../../../raw/book/pages/page_015.md)). These
look identical at the start of the range to a fresh accumulation or
distribution — disambiguating them is one of the methodology's harder
judgment calls.

### Fractality

A complete cycle (all four stages observed) occurs on every time frame
([book p.16](../../../raw/book/pages/page_016.md)). Different time frames can
be in different stages simultaneously. The book treats multi-timeframe
context as mandatory: correct analysis cannot happen without locating the
market in its cycle on the time frame being traded.

## Why It Matters For Wyckoff Reading

- The cycle anchors every label that follows. A spring during accumulation
  is constructive; a "spring" during a redistribution range is a trap. The
  same event has opposite meaning depending on cycle position.
- The book's practical takeaway: identifying the stage correctly may not
  show you *how* to take the trend, but it will keep you off the wrong
  side ([book p.15](../../../raw/book/pages/page_015.md)).
- The stage you are in determines which **position**
  ([[buying-selling-neutral-position]]) you can take.
