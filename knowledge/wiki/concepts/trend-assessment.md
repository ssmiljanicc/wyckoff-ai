# Trend Assessment — Speed, Projection, Depth, Channels

## Summary

Once a [[market-cycle]] stage has been identified as a trend, the book
provides a comparative toolkit for judging that trend's health: **speed**,
**projection**, **depth**, and **lines / channels**
([book p.20–32](../../raw/book/pages/page_020.md)). The unifying principle:
trend health is read by **comparison to prior moves**, never by absolute
levels — "the best way to assess current strength/weakness is to compare
it with previously developed movements"
([book p.20](../../raw/book/pages/page_020.md)).

A weakness in the price does not by itself imply a change of trend; it is
"simply a sign of loss of strength" and is a warning to be prepared for
future movements ([book p.20](../../raw/book/pages/page_020.md)).

## Key Points

### Trend Definition

A trend is the line of least resistance — price moves from one point to
another along the path of least resistance, and the trader's job is to
identify it and trade in harmony with it
([book p.17](../../raw/book/pages/page_017.md)). Three categories
([book p.17–18](../../raw/book/pages/page_017.md)):

- **Bullish** — series of rising highs and rising lows
- **Bearish** — series of falling highs and falling lows
- **Sideways** — highs and lows fluctuate within a range

Trends are also classified by duration into long, medium, and short term —
relatively, not by fixed clock units. The short-term trend lives inside the
medium-term trend, which lives inside the long-term trend
([book p.18](../../raw/book/pages/page_018.md)).

### Speed

The angle at which price moves. Faster than past moves = strength; slower
= weakness ([book p.21](../../raw/book/pages/page_021.md)). Read against
the trend grade you are working on.

### Projection

The distance impulses travel. Two measurements
([book p.22](../../raw/book/pages/page_022.md)):

- **Impulse distance** — current impulse compared to the previous impulse
  going the same way.
- **Distance between extremes** — current high compared to the prior high
  (or current low to prior low).

In a healthy trend each impulse should surpass the previous impulse. An
impulse that fails to make new progress is an alert that the move may be
reaching its end.

### Depth

The distance reactions travel. Mirror of projection, applied to the
counter-trend moves ([book p.23](../../raw/book/pages/page_023.md)):

- **Pullback distance** — shorter pullbacks = strength; longer = weakness.
- **Distance between ends** — comparing successive reaction endpoints.

### Lines

Lines are visual aids for the analyses above. The book is firm: lines must
be drawn with judgment and not indiscriminately — "you must be careful not
to draw lines indiscriminately, especially on every minor movement"
([book p.24](../../raw/book/pages/page_024.md)). More touches = stronger
validity. A break is not automatically a reversal; **how** it breaks and
**what conditions** precede it determine significance
([book p.27](../../raw/book/pages/page_027.md)).

- **Horizontal lines** — connect two or more lows for support, two or more
  highs for resistance. A line that has served both is a **shaft line**;
  broken resistance becomes support and vice versa
  ([book p.25](../../raw/book/pages/page_025.md)).
- **Trend lines** — in a downtrend, connect two decreasing highs (the
  **supply line**, where sellers are expected to reappear). In an uptrend,
  connect two rising lows (the **demand line**, where buyers are expected)
  ([book p.26](../../raw/book/pages/page_026.md)). A line with too much
  slope will break too soon and was not correctly drawn.
- **Channels** — drag the demand line parallel to the intervening high to
  produce the **overbought line**; drag the supply line parallel to the
  intervening low to produce the **oversold line**
  ([book p.28](../../raw/book/pages/page_028.md)). Penetrations of these
  outer lines indicate overbought or oversold conditions and usually
  guide price to a corrective action against the trend.
- **Inverted lines** — in fast moves with no clear trend yet, build the
  opposite-side line first, then derive the trend line from it
  ([book p.29–30](../../raw/book/pages/page_029.md)).
- **Converging lines** — when standard channels do not contain price,
  draw an overbought line from two highs and an oversold line from two
  lows independently of the trend line. Inability to even reach the
  original outer line is itself a sign of weakness (or strength, mirror)
  ([book p.31–32](../../raw/book/pages/page_031.md)).

### Emotional Drivers

Bull trends are driven by greed, leading to overbought conditions; bear
trends are driven by fear, leading to oversold conditions
([book p.19](../../raw/book/pages/page_019.md), [p.29](../../raw/book/pages/page_029.md)).
The book treats these as predictable extremes that the comparative
toolkit is designed to spot before they exhaust.

## Why It Matters For Wyckoff Reading

- Speed / projection / depth give you the comparative vocabulary the book
  insists on — "stronger impulse than prior," "deeper reaction than the
  one before," etc.
- Channel exhaustion is the trend-stage analog of climax events inside a
  range: where the methodology expects the path of least resistance to
  change.
- Trend lines are inputs to [[effort-and-result]] reading at key levels:
  approach a line with volume and an effective break = harmony; approach
  with volume and a false break = divergence
  ([book p.68](../../raw/book/pages/page_068.md)).
