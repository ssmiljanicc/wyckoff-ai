# Law of Effort and Result

## Summary

In financial markets, **effort is volume** and **result is price progress**
([book p.61](../../../raw/book/pages/page_061.md)). The law states that the
price action must reflect the volume action: without effort, no genuine
result can have been produced.

The Wyckoff trader assesses dominance by reading **harmony** or
**divergence** between the two — harmony confirms continuation, divergence
warns of absorption or exhaustion
([book p.62](../../../raw/book/pages/page_062.md)).

## Key Points

### Harmony And Divergence

A significant volume spike indicates the presence of professional money
acting with the aim of producing a movement — continuation or reversal
([book p.62](../../../raw/book/pages/page_062.md)).

- **Harmony** — effort and result agree: wide range on heavy volume
  continuing in the same direction; narrow range on light volume during a
  reaction. Suggests continuation.
- **Divergence** — effort and result disagree: heavy volume producing
  little price progress, or light volume producing an outsized move.
  Suggests the move is being absorbed or that the level was undefended.

Proportionality also holds inside harmony: a larger effort with harmony
produces a longer move; a small effort produces a shorter one
([book p.62](../../../raw/book/pages/page_062.md)).

### Five Reading Scales

The book applies the law at five progressively larger scales
([book p.63–68](../../../raw/book/pages/page_063.md)):

1. **Within a single bar** — wide range should come with proportional
   volume; narrow range with low volume
   ([book p.64](../../../raw/book/pages/page_064.md)).
2. **On the next bar** — does the volume produce continuation in the
   direction of the original candle, or a reversal?
   ([book p.65](../../../raw/book/pages/page_065.md))
3. **Across complete movements** — impulses should print on rising volume,
   reactions on declining volume
   ([book p.66](../../../raw/book/pages/page_066.md)).
4. **By waves** — David Weis's wave-volume tool measures total volume per
   up- and down-wave; compare each wave to the previous wave going the same
   way and to the opposing wave
   ([book p.67](../../../raw/book/pages/page_067.md)).
5. **At key levels** — approach to a level with volume + effective break =
   harmony; approach with volume + false break = divergence
   ([book p.68](../../../raw/book/pages/page_068.md)).

### Effort / Result In Trends

After a long trend, a relative volume spike with small price advance often
marks the end. After a long downtrend it may be a selling climax beginning
an accumulation; after a long uptrend it may be a buying climax beginning a
distribution ([book p.69](../../../raw/book/pages/page_069.md)).

### Lack Of Interest

Turns do not always come from a climactic effort. They can also come from
absence: small volumes on a floor after a long decline indicate the
selling pressure has dried up; small volumes on a ceiling after a long
advance indicate buying pressure has dried up
([book p.69–70](../../../raw/book/pages/page_069.md)). In that state, even a
modest opposing initiative is enough to turn the market.

Note: "lack of interest" appears in both [[supply-and-demand]] (the
mechanism) and here (the diagnostic).

## Why It Matters For Wyckoff Reading

- Volume divergence on a [[significant-bar]] is one of the strongest
  reasons to mistrust an apparent breakout.
- A [[events/spring]] is read primarily through effort/result on the test
  that follows: low volume on the test = supply has dried, demand is in
  control.
- Phase-A climaxes ([[events/selling-climax]], [[events/buying-climax]])
  are diagnosed by a relative volume spike with poor result in the
  direction of the prior trend.
- The Weis wave is a continuous effort/result readout — useful for
  comparing impulses without committing to single-bar reads.
