# Action — Test — Confirmation

## Summary

A recurring three-step pattern runs through every Wyckoff structure:

1. An **action** — a price event that purports to mark a transition
   (break, shake, climax).
2. A **test** — a subsequent probe of the same area with reduced
   volume and narrower ranges.
3. A **confirmation** — the test's result, which validates or
   invalidates the action.

The pattern is not a specific event; it is **the underlying logic of
how every structural transition is verified**. Every Wyckoff event
that "counts" goes through some version of this sequence
([book p.129–130](../../../raw/book/pages/page_129.md);
[book p.167–168](../../../raw/book/pages/page_167.md)).

## Key Points

### The Three Steps

**Action.** The thing that happened: a climax, a breakout, a shake, a
SOS bar. The action **asserts** a structural meaning ("this is the
top", "this is the breakout", "this is the spring") but the assertion
is not yet evidence.

**Test.** A subsequent move that probes the same area to check whether
the asserted meaning holds. The cleanest tests print as **No Demand**
(in a bullish context) or **No Supply** (in a bearish context) —
narrow-range bars with volume lower than the prior two bars
([book p.131](../../../raw/book/pages/page_131.md)).

**Confirmation.** The test's outcome. A test that prints with
narrowing ranges and decreasing volume **confirms** the action. A test
that prints with wide ranges and increasing volume **invalidates** the
action.

### Examples Through Phases

| Phase | Action | Test | Confirmation |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | [[selling-climax]] | [[secondary-test]] | Phase A complete; range begins |
| B | [[st-as-msos]] (UA) | next probe of the high | UA confirmed / refuted |
| C | [[spring]] | spring test | spring confirmed; SOS begins |
| C | [[upthrust-after-distribution]] | UTAD test | UTAD confirmed; SOW begins |
| D | [[sign-of-strength]] | [[back-up-to-the-edge-of-the-creek]] / [[last-point-of-support]] | accumulation confirmed |
| D | [[sign-of-weakness]] | [[fall-through-the-ice]] / [[last-point-of-supply]] | distribution confirmed |
| E | Phase E impulse | trend pullback test | trend continues |

Every row is the same logical pattern.

### Test Validity Criteria

A test is **valid** when ([book p.129](../../../raw/book/pages/page_129.md)):

> "Volume is now low, which clearly indicates lack of interest and
> suggests that the market is now prepared for a trend movement in
> favor of less resistance."

A test is **invalid** (or "poor quality") when:

> "Volume is still high (relatively), which would indicate that there
> are still traders willing to keep pushing the price."

In the invalid case the resolution is to wait for repeated tests
until exhaustion is confirmed.

### Why "Confirmation" Matters Operationally

The book's clearest statement on the operational implication
([book p.154](../../../raw/book/pages/page_154.md)):

> "This breakout movement itself is not an opportunity to trade; it
> only alerts us to a possible opportunity in the very near future.
> This opportunity is found in the immediate action, in the
> confirmation test."

This is the practical anti-FOMO rule. Don't trade the action; trade
the confirmation. The action attracts attention but exposes the
trader to fake-outs. The confirmation has structural conviction.

### Connection To Other Concepts

- **Volume and range diagnostics:** a valid test is a [[effort-and-result]]
  reading — low effort (volume) producing little result, where in a
  trending context that would have produced expansion.
- **Significant bar:** the [[significant-bar]] concept gives the
  diagnostic for an **action** bar (the SOS/SOW Bar). A test bar is the
  inverse — low volume, narrow range, often a No Demand / No Supply
  signature.
- **Labeling discipline:** confirmation is the **license** to label.
  Before confirmation, every label is provisional — see
  [[labeling-is-last-step]].

## Why It Matters For Wyckoff Reading

- The action-test-confirmation pattern is the **shape** of every
  structural transition. Recognizing the pattern lets you predict the
  next event (after an action, expect a test; after a test, expect
  confirmation or refutation).
- The discipline "trade the confirmation, not the action" is one of
  the methodology's most reliable rules of thumb.
- A "missed test" — an action with no test before the next move — is a
  warning. It happens (UTAD without a test, see
  [[upthrust-after-distribution]]) but most often the right move is to
  wait for the test.

## Cross-Author Readings

### As Used By Bruce Fraser (bruce_fraser)

Fraser names the recurring unit "Action and Test". In his wording, the action
has volatility, wide spread, and high volume, while the test should show
compression of price spread, less volatility, and diminishing volume
([Fraser, "Action - Test"](../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2016-09-action--test.md)).

He applies the same two-step pattern across timeframes and treats test quality
as the evidence for whether a climax or breakout has exhausted the prior trend
or still needs more testing
([Fraser, "Action - Test"](../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2016-09-action--test.md)).
