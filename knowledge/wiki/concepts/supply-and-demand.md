# Law of Supply and Demand

## Summary

Wyckoff applies the economic law of supply and demand to financial markets
with one critical refinement: price does not rise because there are "more
buyers than sellers." In any matched market there is always the same number
of buyers and sellers — for someone to buy, someone must sell to them
([book p.47](../../../raw/book/pages/page_047.md)).

What actually moves price is **initiative**: aggressive market orders that
consume the available passive book on the other side. Price rises when
aggressive demand exhausts available supply at one level and is forced to
trade with sellers at a higher level. Price falls when aggressive supply
exhausts available demand and is forced to trade lower
([book p.50](../../../raw/book/pages/page_050.md)).

## Key Points

### Passive vs Aggressive

The book is careful to distinguish two roles
([book p.48](../../../raw/book/pages/page_048.md)):

- **Supply / Demand** (in the technical sense) — passive limit orders resting
  in the BID (demand) and ASK (supply) columns. They represent intention.
- **Sellers / Buyers** — traders who take initiative by hitting the BID
  (selling to market) or lifting the ASK (buying to market).

Passive orders can **stop** a movement but cannot **create** one
([book p.49](../../../raw/book/pages/page_049.md)). Only initiative can move
price.

### Sources of Initiative

An upward price move comes from either
([book p.50](../../../raw/book/pages/page_050.md)):

1. Aggressive buyers lifting the ask, or
2. Stop-loss executions on short positions (which become market buys).

The same logic in mirror applies to downward moves
([book p.51](../../../raw/book/pages/page_051.md)). This is why stop-runs around
range extremes are so productive for the operator — they generate the
counterparty liquidity that lets large positions be filled.

### Lack of Interest

Initiative is not the only thing that can move price. **Absence of opposing
interest** can move it with very little effort
([book p.52](../../../raw/book/pages/page_052.md)). When sellers withdraw from
the ASK, even modest buying pressure walks the price up; when buyers
withdraw from the BID, even modest selling pressure walks it down.

This is why low-volume drifts into resistance or support can still mean
something — and why the absence of volume on a test is a piece of evidence,
not a non-event.

## Why It Matters For Wyckoff Reading

- Aggressive demand is what creates a [[significant-bar]] to the upside;
  aggressive supply creates a downside one.
- Stop-runs are visible as [[events/spring]] and [[events/upthrust]] —
  manoeuvres that manufacture initiative for the operator.
- The **path of least resistance** ([[path-of-least-resistance]]) is the
  direction in which opposing interest is verifiably weak.
- The two readable outputs of this auction are **price** and **volume**
  ([book p.53](../../../raw/book/pages/page_053.md)) — the inputs to
  [[effort-and-result]].
