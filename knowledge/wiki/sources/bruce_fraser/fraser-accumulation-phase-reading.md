## What This Source Group Is

This group covers Fraser's accumulation-side phase reading: stopping action,
absorption, Spring/LPS tests, Jump Across the Creek, and BUEC confirmation. It
adds applied Fraser emphasis to existing pages such as [[accumulation]],

## Key Claims

- Fraser's stopping-action article treats PS, Selling Climax, Automatic Rally,
  and Secondary Test as the four points that show markdown has stopped, while
  warning that this is still too early to call accumulation complete
  ([Stopping Action](../../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2015-05-the-stopping-of-a-downtrend.md)).
- His accumulation article puts absorption at the center: the C.O. keeps prices
  low and listless while buying available shares, and a range without active
  absorption can remain only a trendless range
  ([Absorbing Stock](../../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2015-06-accumulation-phase-absorbing-stock-like-a-sponge.md)).
- Fraser's Creek article describes overhanging supply as a meandering Creek;
  the JAC requires widening price bars and volume, and the later BUEC tests the
  old resistance from above
  ([Jumping the Creek](../../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2015-06-jumping-the-creek.md)).
- His buy-strategy posts put Phase C into the position-building zone: a Spring
  or LPS is the final test of support before markup, followed by SOS/BUEC
  opportunities when demand proves itself
  ([Best Trade Entry Points](../../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2016-04-how-to-determine-the-best-trade-entry-points.md);
  [Wyckoff Buy Strategies](../../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2016-04-wyckoff-buy-strategies.md)).
- The trade-stalking article frames entries as staged commitments: Spring/Test,
  LPS, and BUEC are separate decision points, with later additions made only
  when the earlier position is working
  ([Stalking the Trade](../../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2016-04-stalking-the-trade.md)).
- In the spring article, Fraser distinguishes a high-volume Spring #2 that
  requires a lower-volume test from a low-volume Spring #3 that can turn
  without a separate test
  ([Springing into Action](../../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2016-05-springing-into-action.md)).
- The MU/TWTR case studies show phase labels as conditional: if a supposed
  Phase D rally cannot hold and supply expands, Fraser says the read can be
  relabeled as extended Phase B with final testing still ahead
  ([Phase Analysis](../../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2016-08-phase-analysis-two-case-studies.md)).
- Fraser's Action-Test article treats action and test as a recurring two-step
  process across timeframes; action has wide spread, volatility, and high
  volume, while a good test compresses spread, volatility, and volume
  ([Action - Test](../../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2016-09-action--test.md)).
