# Bruce Fraser - Market Internals And Sentiment

## What This Source Group Is

These posts preserve Fraser use of breadth, sentiment, option activity, and cross-market environmental evidence. They are context filters, not standalone triggers.

## Key Claims

- Fraser treats the percentage of NYSE stocks above the 200-day average as a breadth gauge that behaves differently in bull and bear markets.
  ([Does This Market Have Bad Breadth?](../../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2018-05-does-this-market-have-bad-breadth.md)).
- The same breadth article uses deterioration ahead of price tops as a warning condition, while still reading the 2018 setup as consistent with a bull-market correction.
  ([Does This Market Have Bad Breadth?](../../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2018-05-does-this-market-have-bad-breadth.md)).
- Sentiment Speaks describes sentiment as environmental: useful for understanding speculation, but difficult for precise timing.
  ([Sentiment Speaks](../../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2020-12-sentiment-speaks-806.md)).
- The sentiment post reads extreme call buying and low put/call readings contrary to the crowd, as a sign that bullish speculation has become crowded.
  ([Sentiment Speaks](../../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2020-12-sentiment-speaks-806.md)).
- No Free Lunches keeps environmental conditions behind the trend itself; Fraser says not to put backdrop conditions ahead of the current trend.
  ([There are No Free Lunches](../../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2016-12-there-are-no-free-lunches.md)).
