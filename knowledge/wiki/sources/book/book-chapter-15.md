# Book Chapter 15 — Event #2: Climax

## What This Chapter Is

Defines the **Selling Climax (SC)** for accumulation and **Buying
Climax (BC)** for distribution. The climax is event #2 — the final
stop attempt that defines one structural extreme of the range.

Pages: `raw/book/pages/page_101.md`–`page_111.md`.

## Why It Matters

This is the chapter that introduces the **Climax of Exhaustion**
variants ([p.107–108, p.111](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_107.md))
— the no-volume / low-volume way for a climax to print. Critical
because the textbook "wide bar with volume spike" is only one of two
ways a climax can resolve, and the exhaustion variant is more subtle.

It also gives the **two post-climax paths**: a reaction (which leads
to a range) or a lateral movement (which continues the prior trend).
The reaction is the structural path; this is the first place the
book makes explicit that not every "climax" actually stops the trend
([p.102](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_102.md)).

## Key Claims

- The climax appears after the [[events/preliminary-support|PS]] and
  is followed by the [[events/automatic-rally|AR]] and
  [[events/secondary-test|ST]] in Phase A
  ([p.101](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_101.md)).
- PS sometimes does **not** appear — the climax performs both
  functions ([p.101](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_101.md)). Hence the
  insistence on giving the market flexibility.
- Three valid representations: wide bar, narrow-bar cluster, single
  bar with wick ([p.103](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_103.md)).
- VSA connection: the climax bar is "No Supply" (bullish reversal) or
  "No Demand" (bearish reversal)
  ([p.102](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_102.md)).
- Two post-climax paths
  ([p.102](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_102.md)): reaction → range;
  lateral → trend continuation.
- The SC low defines the **lower limit of the trading range**
  ([p.107](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_107.md)). The BC high defines
  the **upper limit** ([p.110](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_110.md)).
- BC is a **trap** — apparent aggressive buying that is actually
  professional selling absorbing weak-hand demand
  ([p.110](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_110.md)).
- **Climax of Exhaustion** — a trend can end without climactic volume.
  Selling Exhaustion (low-volume SC) is preceded by repeated lower
  PS attempts ([p.108](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_108.md));
  Buying Exhaustion is the mirror ([p.111](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_111.md)).
- Operational use ([p.105](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_105.md)): stop
  trading the prior trend, last profit-taking opportunity. Not an
  entry — experienced traders may take a scalp to the AR.
- The climax low/high is not necessarily the structural extreme — the
  later Phase C shake may probe beyond
  ([p.102](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_102.md)).
