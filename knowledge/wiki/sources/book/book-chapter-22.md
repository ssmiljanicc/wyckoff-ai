# Book Chapter 22 — Phase B: Building the Cause

## What This Chapter Is

The shortest phase chapter. Names Phase B's function (cause-building),
lists its events, and gives the **proportional duration** rule
relative to Phases A and C.

Pages: `raw/book/pages/page_182.md`–`page_183.md`.

## Why It Matters

This chapter is the source of the **proportional duration** guideline
([p.183](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_183.md)) — one of the few
quantitative-ish rules in the methodology. Phase B is normally **the
longest** phase of the structure; when it is shorter, the operator's
urgency adds **strength** to the subsequent trend move.

It also formalizes the Phase B event family as **repeated Secondary
Tests in B** with the four labels (UA, UT, ST as SOW, mSOW) — which
the Chapter 17 material had already introduced informally.

## Key Claims

- After the ST, Phase B begins; its intention is **construction of
  the cause** to prepare the subsequent effect
  ([p.182](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_182.md)).
- Phase B events: **UA + UT** (upper-end tests), **ST as SOW + mSOW**
  (lower-end tests)
  ([p.182](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_182.md)).
- During Phase B the market is **in balance**; the operator absorbs
  most of the stock here
  ([p.182](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_182.md)).
- **Proportional duration rule** ([p.183](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_183.md)):
  Phase B should be **greater than Phases A and C** in duration. A
  general guideline — exceptions exist (V-turns, hypodermic moves)
  but most ranges follow the rule.
- If Phase B is **shorter** than A or C, it denotes **urgency** by
  the operator absorbing stock — adds **strength** to the trend
  move that follows
  ([p.183](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_183.md)).
