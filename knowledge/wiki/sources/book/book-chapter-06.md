# Book Chapter 6 — Wyckoff Methodology Structures

## What This Chapter Is

Presents the four basic schematics: accumulation #1 (with spring),
accumulation #2 (no shake), distribution #1 (with UTAD), distribution #2
(no shake). The chapter is mostly captioned diagrams that name every
event for the first time.

Pages: `raw/book/pages/page_037.md`–`page_045.md`.

## Why It Matters

This is where the complete event vocabulary (PS, SC, AR, ST, spring,
LPS, UA, SOS, BU/BUEC, JAC, plus their distribution mirrors PSY, BC,
ST, UT, UTAD, MSOW, LPSY) is introduced on a single canvas with the
phases A–E labeled. Every later chapter on events refers back to these
diagrams.

It is also where the **CHoCH** (Change of Character) and the
**creek / ice** levels are named — creek for accumulation resistance,
ice for distribution support.

## Key Claims

- Markets are alive; using fixed patterns to read context is a mistake
  ([p.37](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_037.md)).
- The methodology's **fixed elements are the events and the phases**;
  the structures are templates
  ([p.37](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_037.md)).
- Two variants of each structure exist — one with a terminal shake
  (spring / UTAD), one without
  ([p.40–41](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_040.md),
  [p.44–45](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_044.md)).
- In the no-shake variants, the primary trade opportunity moves to the
  Phase-D breakout test ([[events/back-up-to-the-edge-of-the-creek|BUEC]]
  for accumulation, [[events/last-point-of-supply|LPSY]] for
  distribution) ([p.41, 45](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_041.md)).
