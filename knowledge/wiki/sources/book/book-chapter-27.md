# Book Chapter 27 — Decision-Making

## What This Chapter Is

Chapter 27 is the operational decision layer after the prior chapters have
defined structures, events, and phases. Its stated goal is to add objectivity
to graph reading and, especially, to trading decisions
([p.208](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_208.md)).

The chapter covers [[significant-bar]], [[reversal-of-movement]], position
management, position sizing, entry execution, stop placement, and
take-profit logic.

## Why It Matters

This chapter connects the Wyckoff read to order placement. It specifies the
trigger bar, the invalidation point, and the exit evidence instead of leaving
the trader with a correct structure read but no execution discipline.

The important constraint is that the trigger must appear at the right
contextual area. The book gives the example of a potential LPSY after a
bearish break: a significant downward wing at the Ice, closing below the
last significant upward wing, can provide the trading opportunity
([p.215](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_215.md)).

## Key Claims

- A significant bar has wider range, higher/proportional volume, a close
  in the direction of movement, and commitment through a relevant level
  ([p.208–209](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_208.md)).
- If one clean significant bar does not form, two normal bars can be read
  together as a composite significant bar
  ([p.210](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_210.md)).
- Movement reversal is defined by an opposing significant bar closing
  beyond the last significant bar that marked current control
  ([p.210–211](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_210.md)).
- Entry, stop loss, and take profit should be sent together to avoid later
  electronic or emotional failure
  ([p.212–213](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_212.md)).
- Position size should be calculated from the distance between entry and
  stop; the author's example uses fixed account-risk sizing and recommends
  not exceeding 1% risk per position
  ([p.214](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_214.md)).
- Stop loss belongs where the scenario is invalidated, generally on the
  other side of the significant bar and the whole scenario
  ([p.216–217](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_216.md)).
- For shake-test and minor-structure entries, stop placement shifts
  between the significant bar and the whole scenario, depending on the
  entry type ([p.218](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_218.md)).
- For break-test entries, the stop belongs beyond the significant bar and
  broken Creek/Ice; the chapter also discusses trailing stops as an optional
  management method ([p.220](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_220.md)).
- Take-profit evidence can come from climatic bars, the development of
  Phase A against the position, liquidity zones, or volume-profile levels
  ([p.221–226](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_221.md)).
