# Book Chapter 11 — Reaccumulation

## What This Chapter Is

The shortest of the cycle-position chapters. Argues that reaccumulation
is mechanically identical to accumulation — the only difference is that
the prior move was up rather than down. The chapter focuses on
disambiguation: how to avoid confusing reaccumulation with distribution,
since both start the same way (after a stop of an upward move).

Pages: `raw/book/pages/page_077.md`–`page_079.md`.

## Why It Matters

Reaccumulation is one of the two "hardest reads in the methodology" —
the one that determines whether you stay long in the existing trend or
flip short. The chapter is short because the mechanics are identical to
Chapter 10, but the diagnostic challenge gets its own page.

This chapter's wiki output is in Batch 2
([[structures/reaccumulation]]). Batch 1 captures the cause-effect
duration claim.

## Key Claims

- Reaccumulation = accumulation mechanically; only difference is the
  initial trend direction
  ([p.77](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_077.md)).
- During an uptrend, control gradually shifts from strong to weak hands;
  reaccumulation rebuilds strong-hand control
  ([p.78](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_078.md)).
- Structure duration is influenced by the strong/weak hand mix: if value
  is still mostly in strong hands, the structure is shorter; if mostly
  in weak hands, it must be longer
  ([p.78](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_078.md)).
- Reaccumulation and distribution both start after the stop of an
  upward move — disambiguating them is "one of the most compromising
  situations that any Wyckoff trader will encounter"
  ([p.79](../../../../raw/book/pages/page_079.md)).
