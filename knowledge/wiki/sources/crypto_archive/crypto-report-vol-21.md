# Crypto Report Vol 21

## What This Source Is

Vol 21 is the archive's clearest Bitcoin campaign note in this batch. It
names the three stages of the uptrend, treats halving as a catalyst, and
compares the 2020 shock to the 2017 China-ban shock
([vol 21](../../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-21.html)).

## Key Claims

- The report labels the 2020 uptrend sequence as value buying, absorption,
  and momentum, with the halving event acting as a catalyst
  ([vol 21](../../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-21.html)).
- It says Bitcoin had outperformed the S&P since the March selloff, while a
  further push could force short sellers to capitulate and create liquidity
  for larger operators
  ([vol 21](../../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-21.html)).
- The long-term accumulation recap maps the two-year Bitcoin structure
  through stopping action, low institutional participation, extreme oversold
  value buying, profit taking, early absorption, margin-call selloff,
  [[spring|shakeout]], potential [[sign-of-strength]], and future [[last-point-of-support]]
  ([vol 21](../../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-21.html)).
- The daily sequence is described as supply, absorption, feather, and
  acceleration, repeating as upper supply zones show less volume
  ([vol 21](../../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-21.html)).
- The 2017 China-ban analog treats the shock as exogenous: Bitcoin became a
  funding source, value buyers stepped in, volatility contracted, and futures
  later acted as a catalyst
  ([vol 21](../../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-21.html)).
