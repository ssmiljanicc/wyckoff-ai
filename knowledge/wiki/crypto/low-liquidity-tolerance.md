# Low Liquidity Tolerance

## Summary

**Low-liquidity tolerance** is the archive's practical adjustment for crypto:
trust major-exchange volume more than small venues, expect weekend thinness,
and manage Bitcoin campaigns swing by swing because volatility is structurally
high.

## Key Points

- Vol 35 says crypto volume was historically unreliable on small exchanges, but
  major venues such as Binance, Coinbase, Kraken, FTX, Bitstamp, and Bitmex
  had reliable data and handled most price discovery
  ([vol 35](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-35.html)).
- The same Q&A recommends aggregated data such as Messari to monitor better
  volume inputs
  ([vol 35](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-35.html)).
- Vol 44 warns that sudden Bitcoin selling can appear on weekends, especially
  Saturday, because crypto is generally thin on weekends
  ([vol 44](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-44.html)).
- Vol 48 says Bitcoin's volatility forces traders to "stalk the uptrend" and
  pay attention to each swing to reduce portfolio volatility and allocate
  capital efficiently
  ([vol 48](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-48.html)).

## Why It Matters For Crypto Reading

- It narrows which volume data should carry evidence weight.
- It makes weekend breakdowns and shakeouts possible without treating every
  sharp move as a completed [[reversal-of-movement]].
- It links execution discipline to [[significant-bar]],
  [[reversal-of-movement]], and [[action-test-confirmation]].
