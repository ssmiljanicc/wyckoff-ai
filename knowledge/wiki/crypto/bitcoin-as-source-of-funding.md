# Bitcoin As Source Of Funding

## Summary

**Bitcoin as source of funding** is the archive's warning that Bitcoin can be
sold for liquidity during cross-asset stress. In those moments, Bitcoin does
not behave only as an independent crypto asset; it can become cash collateral
for institutions or ecosystem participants that need to meet margin or business
needs.

## Key Points

- Vol 14 attributes the March 2020 capitulation to cash shortages from margin
  calls that forced institutions to sell many assets, including Bitcoin
  ([vol 14](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-14.html)).
- Vol 15 keeps the same attribution by reading the upper half of the
  capitulation bar as the area where margin calls forced Bitcoin selling
  ([vol 15](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-15.html)).
- Vol 21 repeats the mechanism at two time scales: the 2020 global selloff
  brought Bitcoin back to support, and the 2017 China-ban shock made Bitcoin
  a funding source while miners and crypto companies had to restructure
  ([vol 21](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-21.html)).
- The concept is not a new Wyckoff event. It is a crypto-specific cause for
  why a [[spring|shakeout]], [[selling-climax]], or large change-of-behavior can
  occur across assets at the same time.

## Why It Matters For Crypto Reading

- It prevents treating Bitcoin strength as guaranteed during panic. In a
  liquidation wave, Bitcoin may be sold because it is liquid and usable as a
  funding source.
- It links crypto analysis to the [[crypto/intermarket-gate]]: equity stress
  can matter because the same actors may be forced to reduce exposure across
  markets.
- It makes the recovery read more important than the drop label. The archive
  waits for value buying, reduced supply, higher lows, and confirmation before
  treating the shock as constructive.
