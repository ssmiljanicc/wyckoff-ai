# Comparative Strength

## Summary

**Comparative strength** is the archive's selection discipline for crypto. It
compares Bitcoin to equities, leaders to Bitcoin, or crypto indexes to one
another by synchronizing significant highs/lows and judging which instrument
absorbs supply and advances better.

## Key Points

- Vol 19 uses BTC/SPX relative strength: the RS line had formed a base and
  hinge, and a higher low plus trendline break would support a long-term
  bullish Bitcoin scenario
  ([vol 19](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-19.html)).
- Vol 20 notes that BTC/SPX had finally broken its downtrend and that a
  higher-low retest would be constructive
  ([vol 20](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-20.html)).
- Vol 20 also compares ChainLink and Bitcoin by projecting significant highs
  and lows from the market mover to Bitcoin, then observing which chart rallies
  better and reacts more shallowly
  ([vol 20](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-20.html)).
- Vol 22 states the method directly: synchronize significant lows and highs,
  then compare slopes to choose the best performer
  ([vol 22](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-22.html)).
- Vol 25 expands the comparison to sector indexes: Bitcoin, large caps,
  mid caps, and micro caps
  ([vol 25](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-25.html)).

## Why It Matters For Crypto Reading

- It prevents treating all crypto charts as equivalent when the broad tape is
  bullish.
- It makes leadership visible before the USD pair produces an obvious
  breakout.
- It complements [[crypto/spread-charts]] and [[crypto/rotation-hierarchy]] by
  turning rotation into a repeatable selection process.
