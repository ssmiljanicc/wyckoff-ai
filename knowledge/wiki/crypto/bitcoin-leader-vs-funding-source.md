# Bitcoin Leader Vs Funding Source

## Summary

The archive treats Bitcoin as a changing role, not a permanent buy signal.
During defensive periods it can be a refuge beside Tether; during an advancing
crypto tape it can lead first, then become the funding source for rotation into
more speculative assets.

## Key Points

- In risk-off mode, Vol 36 says crypto institutions usually stay in Tether and
  Bitcoin; for a rally to become more sustainable, capital should flow from
  Bitcoin into mid caps and low caps
  ([vol 36](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-36.html)).
- Vol 38 describes a risk-off to risk-on transition where Bitcoin outperforms
  in the first half of the formation, then underperforms when institutions move
  into more speculative assets
  ([vol 38](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-38.html)).
- Vol 41 keeps the position with Bitcoin while it is the leading asset, but
  prepares a switch if Bitcoin fails and altcoins react less on the reaction
  ([vol 41](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-41.html)).
- Vol 42 describes a terminal-leadership pattern: when Bitcoin is in the
  speculative stage, short-term profit taking can let oversold low caps rally,
  but that is not yet proof of a durable altcoin cycle
  ([vol 42](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-42.html)).

## Why It Matters For Crypto Reading

- It prevents assuming Bitcoin strength is always bullish for every crypto
  asset.
- It links market direction to [[crypto/rotation-hierarchy]] and selection:
  Bitcoin can be the correct holding, or it can be the asset that funds the
  next leadership group.
- It keeps the archive aligned with [[crypto/intermarket-gate]] because the
  final structure is still influenced by S&P/Nasdaq behavior.
