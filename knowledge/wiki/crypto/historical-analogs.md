# Historical Analogs

## Summary

**Historical analogs** in the archive are comparison structures used to frame
what might happen next. They are not predictions; they are scenario templates
that must still pass current [[supply-and-demand]] and [[effort-and-result]]
tests.

## Key Points

- Vol 16 uses the 1987 S&P crash as an analog for quick recovery followed by
  stepping-stone [[reaccumulation]], where volatility and supply unfold slowly
  after the initial recovery
  ([vol 16](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-16.html)).
- Vol 17 studies the 1998 S&P bottom as a climactic action followed by a large
  [[automatic-rally]], an upthrust of the capitulation-bar top, and a later
  double-bottom structure
  ([vol 17](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-17.html)).
- Vol 18 applies an Ethereum 2018-2019 recovery analog to ChainLink: fast
  recovery from oversold, overshoot of supply, then return to support on
  reduced volume and spread
  ([vol 18](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-18.html)).
- Vol 21 uses the 2017 China-ban shock as a crypto-native analog: forced
  selling, Bitcoin as funding source, value buying, restructuring, volatility
  contraction, and later catalyst support
  ([vol 21](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-21.html)).

## Why It Matters For Crypto Reading

- Analogs help form scenarios, but the current chart must still confirm.
- They keep the analyst from forcing every shock into a simple Composite Man
  narrative; Vol 21 explicitly treats exogenous shocks as real constraints.
- They connect crypto archive work to older market studies without importing
  unsupported claims into the wiki.
