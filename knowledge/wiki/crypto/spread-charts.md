# Spread Charts

## Summary

**Spread charts** in the crypto archive are altcoin-versus-Bitcoin pairs such
as LINKBTC or ETHBTC. They are used to detect relative leadership and rotation,
then the actual entry is usually sought on the USD pair.

## Key Points

- Vol 17 says spread charts are popular in crypto and that altcoin-Bitcoin
  pairs can be analyzed with Wyckoff tools; it specifically warns not to trade
  the spread directly when Bitcoin price is falling
  ([vol 17](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-17.html)).
- In the same Vol 17 example, LINKBTC provides the alert, and the analyst then
  switches to LINKUSD to look for the entry
  ([vol 17](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-17.html)).
- Vol 20 treats ETHBTC as an indicator of speculative sentiment, where a
  distributional pattern implies institutions may have locked in profits
  before short-term weakness
  ([vol 20](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-20.html)).
- Vol 22 uses ETHBTC to identify early rotation from Bitcoin to altcoins: a
  base near support and a downtrend break would point to an altcoin-dominated
  upswing
  ([vol 22](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-22.html)).
- Vol 23 confirms the follow-through: ETHBTC had broken the trendline and
  altcoins were outperforming Bitcoin
  ([vol 23](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-23.html)).

## Why It Matters For Crypto Reading

- Spread charts turn crypto selection into a [[comparative-strength]] problem.
- They help avoid buying the wrong asset in a broad crypto rally.
- They do not replace [[supply-and-demand]] on the traded pair; they only point
  to where the setup should be searched.
