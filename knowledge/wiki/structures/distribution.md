# Distribution

## Summary

A **distribution** structure is a lateral movement preceded by a
bullish trend in which large traders absorb available demand from
weak hands in order to short and profit at lower prices in a
subsequent downtrend ([book p.80](../../../raw/book/pages/page_080.md)).

It is the mirror of [[accumulation]]. The defining feature is the
**direction of stock flow**: from strong hands to weak hands during
the range, producing the cause for the subsequent markdown.

The book presents two schematic variants
([book p.42, p.44](../../../raw/book/pages/page_042.md)):

- **Basic Distribution Structure #1** — with a terminal
  [[events/upthrust-after-distribution|UTAD]] in Phase C.
- **Basic Distribution Structure #2** — without a Phase C shake;
  primary trade moves to the [[events/last-point-of-supply|LPSY]].

This page covers what is specific to the distribution read; the
general range theory is in [[trading-range]].

## Key Points

### Stock Control — Mirror Of Accumulation

During the prior uptrend, control of stock gradually transfers from
strong hands to weak hands as price rises. The distribution range is
where the **final** sell-side absorption happens — the operator
absorbs the remaining floating demand at favourable prices and builds
the short position.

For the markdown to begin, weak-hand demand must be exhausted. Same
[[concepts/cause-and-effect|cause-and-effect]] logic applies: range
duration predicts trend magnitude.

### Schematic #1 — With Terminal Shake (UTAD)

**[[concepts/phase-a|Phase A — Stop the previous bullish trend]]:**

- [[events/preliminary-support|Preliminary Supply (PSY)]] — first
  large-trader sell entry.
- [[events/buying-climax|Buying Climax (BC)]] — final stop; defines
  the upper boundary of the range.
- [[events/automatic-reaction|Automatic Reaction (AR)]] — first
  counter-move; defines the lower boundary (the [[concepts/creek-and-ice|Ice]]).
- [[events/secondary-test|Secondary Test (ST)]] — confirms the climax;
  ends Phase A.

First **CHoCh** runs from the BC high to the AR low — transition from
uptrend to range
([book p.42](../../../raw/book/pages/page_042.md)).

**[[concepts/phase-b|Phase B — Cause-building]]:**

- [[events/upthrust|Upthrust (UT)]] — tests of the upper end.
- [[events/st-as-msow|minor Sign of Weakness (mSOW)]] — tests of the
  lower end.

Note the key contrast with accumulation: distribution **remains
volatile** during the entire range, not just at the boundaries
([book p.84](../../../raw/book/pages/page_084.md)).

**[[concepts/phase-c|Phase C — Test]]:**

- [[events/upthrust-after-distribution|UTAD]] — the terminal upward
  shake that grabs the remaining liquidity above the structure. Volume
  is moderate to strong.
- **UTAD test** — pullback that holds below the UTAD high. The test
  is sometimes absent when supply enters too aggressively — see
  [[events/upthrust-after-distribution]].
- Variants: minor UTAD (in-range shake), Terminal Upthrust (extreme
  variant).

**[[concepts/phase-d|Phase D — Trend within range]]:**

- [[events/sign-of-weakness|Major Sign of Weakness (MSOW)]] — the
  bearish break of the Ice. Second CHoCh (Phase C high to SOW low) —
  transition from range to downtrend.
- [[events/last-point-of-supply|LPSY]] — interior corrective waves
  (lower highs).
- [[events/fall-through-the-ice|Fall Through The Ice (FTI)]] — the
  confirmation test after the SOW; **the canonical Wyckoff short
  entry**.

**[[concepts/phase-e|Phase E — Trend out of range]]:**

Succession of SOW impulses and LPSY pullbacks, producing falling
highs and lows.

### Schematic #2 — No-Shake Variant

In Distribution #2 the Phase C shake **does not reach the highs of
the structure** ([book p.44–45](../../../raw/book/pages/page_044.md)).
The book frames it as the inverse of Accumulation #2: greater
background weakness, with already-positioned shorts preventing price
from reaching the upper liquidity zone.

Operational consequences:

- The UTAD entry is **lost**.
- An interior LPSY entry is in doubt because price might still shake
  to the structural high.
- The SOW that breaks the structure is also lost as a discrete entry.
- **The only clean short entry is the [[events/fall-through-the-ice|FTI]]
  / LPSY** after the breakdown
  ([book p.45](../../../raw/book/pages/page_045.md)).

See [[events/no-shake-phase-c]].

### The Handling Maneuver — Bullish News, Bearish Intent

During distribution the operator creates an **environment of extreme
strength**, often supported by the media
([book p.82](../../../raw/book/pages/page_082.md)):

- Bullish news attracts late retail buying.
- Those late buyers provide the demand-side counterparty for the
  operator's sell orders.
- The UTAD (when present) performs the **triple function**: hit
  stops of shorts, induce buying by trend-followers, profit from the
  subsequent markdown.

The book reiterates: not every distribution shows a UTAD; the
no-shake variant is structurally valid.

### Verification — Path Of Least Resistance (Downward)

The operator does not initiate the markdown until the
[[concepts/path-of-least-resistance|path of least resistance]] is
verified downward — by **upward probes** that test for absence of
buyer participation
([book p.83](../../../raw/book/pages/page_083.md)). Hence multiple
shakes within a single distribution range are common.

### Common Characteristics — Crucial Contrast With Accumulation

From [book p.84](../../../raw/book/pages/page_084.md):

- **High volume and volatility throughout the range.** Wide price
  fluctuations; volume stays relatively high and constant. (Contrast:
  accumulation shows **decreasing** volume.)
- **Tests to the low zone with low volume** — no buyer interest,
  except just before the breakdown.
- **Upward shakes to previous highs** — to the structural resistance
  or to minor in-range highs.
- **Wider, more fluid down bars than up bars** — quality supply is
  entering.
- **Lower highs and lower lows in the late stage** — already a
  bearish signature inside the range.

The "high volatility throughout" cue is **one of the most important
diagnostic contrasts** the methodology provides. A range that stays
volatile is much more likely to be distribution than accumulation.

### Common Trading Mistakes (synthesis)

> **Synthesis:** Failure modes implicit in the methodology — not direct
> book claims. Each mistake corresponds to a structural feature cited
> elsewhere on this page (LPSY, Phase A direction ambiguity, no-shake
> variant). Sources: [[book-chapter-06]], [[book-chapter-12]].

- **Shorting the SOW, not the LPSY.** Same as the accumulation
  mistake but mirrored — chasing the breakdown.
- **Committing to a distribution read at the end of Phase A.** Phase
  A is identical for distribution and reaccumulation. Direction is
  inferred during Phase B and confirmed during Phase D.
- **Refusing Distribution #2 setups** because the UTAD is missing.
  The no-shake variant is structurally valid; entry moves to FTI /
  LPSY.

## Why It Matters For Wyckoff Reading

- Distribution is the mirror of accumulation in mechanics but has a
  characteristic **volume/volatility signature** that differentiates
  it. The "stays volatile" rule is one of the methodology's most
  practical diagnostic tools.
- The two-schematic framework handles most real-world distributions;
  Terminal Upthrust and Ordinary Upthrust (see
  [[events/upthrust-after-distribution]]) cover the edge variants.
- The full event vocabulary appears here in its bearish form, mirroring
  the accumulation vocabulary one-for-one.

## Cross-Author Readings

### As Used By Bruce Fraser (bruce_fraser)

Fraser's distribution writing treats phase analysis as context: schematics are
road maps, but each phase has a distinct price/volume character that reveals
how the C.O. selling campaign is maturing
([Fraser, "Context is King"](../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2015-09-context-is-king.md)).

His applied event language focuses on PSY/BCLX/AR/ST/UT/SOW/UTAD/LPSY as
markers in that evolution. Phase B is watched for volume near resistance and
high volume on declines, Phase C resolves through UTAD or LPSY, and Phase D is
where the Ice/support area breaks and gets tested from below
([Fraser, "Distribution Definitions"](../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2015-09-distribution-definitions.md);
[Fraser, "Divining Distribution"](../../../raw/bruce_fraser/posts/articles-wyckoff-2018-03-divining-distribution.md)).
