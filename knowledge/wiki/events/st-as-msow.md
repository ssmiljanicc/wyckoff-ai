# Secondary Test as Sign Of Weakness (ST as SOW / minor SOW)

## Summary

When a Phase B test probes the **lower end** of a range and produces a
**lower low** than the structural support, the book labels it **ST as
Sign Of Weakness (ST as SOW)** in an accumulation read, or **minor
Sign Of Weakness (mSOW)** in a distribution read
([book p.127–128](../../raw/book/pages/page_127.md)). It is the
**lower-end mirror** of the [[st-as-msos|UA / mSOS]] event.

The event denotes fundamental weakness: well-informed traders know the
price is overvalued and have urgency to sell, producing pressure on
the lower boundary.

## Key Points

### What Happens

The price extends through the structural support (initially set by the
[[selling-climax]] / [[automatic-reaction]]), making a **lower low**
than the prior range floor
([book p.127](../../raw/book/pages/page_127.md)). The drive is produced
either by aggressive sellers or by absent buyers — both readings yield
the same: more tests of this area are likely.

The new low defines a fresh support level for the structure — useful
for locating the effective bearish break (in distribution) or the final
shake before the up move (in accumulation).

### ST as SOW vs mSOW — When To Use Which

- **ST as SOW** — used when the range is **accumulation**. The lower
  low is interpreted as a final probe before the structure resolves up
  ([book p.127](../../raw/book/pages/page_127.md)). This event is more
  likely when the Phase A [[secondary-test]] already produced a lower
  low — extreme weakness in the market that needs to be tested.
- **mSOW (minor Sign Of Weakness)** — used when the range is
  **distribution / redistribution**
  ([book p.128](../../raw/book/pages/page_128.md)). A diagnostic clue:
  the Phase A ST was a **poor bullish movement** (little path, lack of
  buyer interest) — that prior weakness foreshadows the mSOW.

### Fallback Label — "ST in B"

The book repeatedly emphasizes that real-time labeling cannot always
disambiguate. The pragmatic suggestion: when direction is unknown,
label the event ambiguously as **"ST in B"** and revise after the
structure resolves ([book p.128](../../raw/book/pages/page_128.md)).
This is consistent with [[concepts/labeling-is-last-step]].

### Diagnostic Role In Distribution

A clean mSOW in Phase B is a strong pre-Phase-C clue that the structure
is **distribution** rather than reaccumulation:

- Sellers were able to push price through support — demand is not
  aggressive enough to defend the low.
- This raises the probability of an
  [[upthrust-after-distribution|UTAD]] (rather than [[spring]]) when
  the Phase C shake develops.

### Subsequent Test

A ST as SOW / mSOW, like all tests, can be retested. A retest of the
same lower boundary with **lower volume and narrower range** confirms
the weakness reading. A retest with **higher volume** suggests demand
is still defending the level — accumulation diagnostics rise.

## Why It Matters For Wyckoff Reading

- A clean mSOW in Phase B **biases the Phase C shake direction toward
  UTAD** rather than spring.
- The decision ST-as-SOW vs mSOW is the lower-end mirror of UA-vs-UT.
  Same action, different label by context — see
  [[concepts/labeling-is-last-step]].
- Together with [[st-as-msos]] (the upper-end mirror), this event
  completes the geography of Phase B testing.
