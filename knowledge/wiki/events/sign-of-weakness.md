# Sign of Weakness (SOW) and Major SOW

## Summary

The **Sign of Weakness (SOW)** — also called **Major SOW (MSOW)** when
range-breaking — is the bearish breakout event (event #6 of the
methodology) in a distribution structure. It is the downward move that
originates from the Phase C high — either the
[[upthrust-after-distribution|UTAD]] or the
[[last-point-of-supply|LPSY]] — and breaks the lower range boundary
(the [[concepts/creek-and-ice|Ice]]) to begin the new bearish trend
([book p.164](../../raw/book/pages/page_164.md)).

The SOW produces the **second Change of Character (CHoCh)** of the
distribution: the market exits the range and enters a markdown phase.

The accumulation mirror is [[sign-of-strength]].

## Key Points

### Where The SOW Originates

The SOW originates in the **high of Phase C**
([book p.164](../../raw/book/pages/page_164.md)):

- In **Distribution #1** (with shake) — from the [[upthrust-after-distribution|UTAD]] high.
- In **Distribution #2** (no shake) — from the
  [[last-point-of-supply|LPSY]] inside the range.

The book notes the breakdown "could be a second, third or fourth attempt
to break the Ice and this is the most successful"
([book p.164](../../raw/book/pages/page_164.md)) — i.e. it is normal
for there to be earlier failed attempts to break the lower boundary
before the MSOW actually succeeds.

### Appearance

A SOW is the bearish mirror of a [[sign-of-strength|SOS]]
([book p.155](../../raw/book/pages/page_155.md)):

- Relative **increase in price ranges** (wide bearish bars).
- **Increase in volume**.
- Fluid breaks of previous liquidity levels (previous lows, the minor
  Ice).

### Quality Of A Major SOW

For the SOW to qualify as Major SOW (MSOW)
([book p.164](../../raw/book/pages/page_164.md)):

- The bearish movement shows **ease of movement** — easy displacement
  through prior levels.
- It covers a **relatively long distance** — at least reaching the
  **midpoint of the range**.
- Any subsequent regression **does not reach the UTAD high** (or the
  LPSY high in Distribution #2).

Failing these conditions, the move is a **minor SOW (mSOW)** — see
[[st-as-msow]] — which can describe weakness inside Phase B or after
Phase C that does not break the structure.

### SOW Bar — The Entry Trigger

The **SOW Bar** is a specific bar pattern used as an entry trigger for
shorts ([book p.166](../../raw/book/pages/page_166.md)):

- Wide bearish bar.
- Close **at the lows** of the range.
- **Increased volume**.
- Alternatively, a **bearish gap**.

It signals seller aggression and is the institutional shorting moment.
When a SOW bar appears in the operative zone (after a shake, after a
break, or in trend), it is the definitive sign that professionals
support the down move.

### Breakout Is Not An Entry Opportunity

Same discipline as SOS
([book p.159](../../raw/book/pages/page_159.md)): the breakdown itself
should **not** be traded. The correct entry is the **confirmation
test** — LPSY / FTI in distribution. See
[[last-point-of-supply]], [[fall-through-the-ice]].

### Failed Variant

If price re-enters the range after the breakdown, the event was an
[[failed-signal|failed SOW]] — the bearish thesis is invalidated and
the next move is typically a SOS / JAC. The signature is identical to
a failed bullish break: the move did not stay outside the boundary.

## Why It Matters For Wyckoff Reading

- The SOW is the **trigger** that confirms the entire distribution
  thesis. Until it prints, every Phase B / Phase C label is provisional.
- Major SOW vs minor SOW is the **structural-vs-intra-range** distinction
  — only a Major SOW validates the distribution at the structure level.
- The "do not enter on the break, wait for the confirmation test"
  discipline applies symmetrically.
