# Last Point of Support (LPS)

## Summary

**Last Point of Support (LPS)** is the **functional name** for any
defended-lower-low in an accumulation structure where buyers step in,
stop the decline, and produce a higher low that precedes a new bullish
impulse ([book p.173](../../raw/book/pages/page_173.md)).

LPS is "the immediate action that precedes a [[sign-of-strength|Sign of
Strength (SOS)]]" — sellers attempt to push price lower but fail; buyers
appear aggressively; a new markup begins.

LPS is **not** a single event with one location — it is a **family of
events** distinguished by where in the structure they occur. The book
catalogs three types
([book p.174](../../raw/book/pages/page_174.md)):

1. **LPS after a shake** — the test of a [[spring]] or Shakeout.
2. **LPS within the range** — the higher low after a Phase B / D
   bullish push, before the next SOS.
3. **LPS out of range** — the [[back-up-to-the-edge-of-the-creek|BUEC]]
   (Phase D confirmation) and Phase E pullbacks during the markup
   trend.

The distribution mirror is [[last-point-of-supply|LPSY]].

## Key Points

### LPS After A Shake

In Accumulation #1, the test of the [[spring]] (or Terminal Shakeout)
**is** the LPS ([book p.174](../../raw/book/pages/page_174.md)).

- The shake produces a deep low.
- The test pulls back and holds **above the shake low** — that test
  level is the LPS.
- The SOS (and eventual JAC of the Creek) starts from this LPS.

Spring #3 is the exception — the spring is itself a clean enough
test that the LPS / SOS can develop directly from the spring low
without an explicit retest (see [[spring]]).

### LPS Within The Range (Accumulation #2 / No-Shake)

In the no-shake variant, the LPS appears **inside the structure** as
the higher low between an interior bullish impulse and the eventual
break of the Creek
([book p.174](../../raw/book/pages/page_174.md)). It is the primary
trade location for Accumulation #2 — see [[no-shake-phase-c]].

This is the LPS that follows an interior [[sign-of-strength|mSOS]]
during Phase B / early Phase D. The trader is positioned at the LPS
expecting the next leg up to break the structure.

### LPS Out Of Range — BUEC + Phase E Pullbacks

Out-of-range LPS appears as
([book p.174](../../raw/book/pages/page_174.md)):

- The **confirmation event after the Phase D breakout** — synonymous
  with [[back-up-to-the-edge-of-the-creek|BUEC]].
- **All pullbacks during Phase E** as the markup unfolds.

In wave terms, every corrective wave during the new uptrend is an LPS
— a place where buyers re-engage before the next impulse. See
[[concepts/waves-and-fractals]].

### Discipline — Buy The LPS, Not The SOS

The book is explicit
([book p.174](../../raw/book/pages/page_174.md)):

> "Many traders guided by their lack of understanding will be buying
> during the development of the strength signal (SOS). But this action
> is not correct, it is best to wait for the next reaction (LPS) at
> that point to start looking for a trigger to enter the market."

Buying into the SOS chases the move; buying at the LPS waits for the
counter-wave and enters at the structural anchor. This is the
trader-discipline implication of [[concepts/waves-and-fractals]].

### Price Coincidence With PS

The LPS price level sometimes coincides with the original
[[preliminary-support|Preliminary Support]] price
([book p.175](../../raw/book/pages/page_175.md)) — the operator
returns to the same area where absorption originally began. This is a
"price magnet" effect: zones with lots of prior crossing orders
remain attractive levels.

## Why It Matters For Wyckoff Reading

- LPS is not one event but a family. Recognizing which type you are
  looking at determines the structural read (Spring test, mid-range
  pullback, BUEC, Phase E pullback).
- The LPS-buy / not-SOS-buy discipline is one of the methodology's
  most reliable rules of thumb.
- LPS = BUEC when the LPS sits as the confirmation event out of the
  range — the two labels overlap.
