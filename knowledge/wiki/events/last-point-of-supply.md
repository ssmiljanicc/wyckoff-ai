# Last Point of Supply (LPSY)

## Summary

**Last Point of Supply (LPSY)** is the **functional name** for any
defended-higher-high in a distribution structure where sellers step in,
block the advance, and produce a lower high that precedes a new bearish
impulse ([book p.176](../../raw/book/pages/page_176.md)).

LPSY is "the immediate action that precedes a [[sign-of-weakness|Sign
Of Weakness (SOW)]]" — buyers attempt to push price higher; large
sellers (already positioned short) appear to defend their positions; a
new markdown begins.

Like its accumulation mirror [[last-point-of-support|LPS]], LPSY is a
**family of events** distinguished by location
([book p.176](../../raw/book/pages/page_176.md)):

1. **LPSY after a shake** — the test of an
   [[upthrust-after-distribution|UTAD]] / Terminal Upthrust.
2. **LPSY within the range** — the lower high after a Phase B / D
   bearish push, before the next SOW.
3. **LPSY out of range** — the [[fall-through-the-ice|FTI]] (Phase D
   confirmation) and Phase E pullbacks during the markdown trend.

## Key Points

### LPSY After A Shake

In Distribution #1, the test of the UTAD is the LPSY
([book p.176](../../raw/book/pages/page_176.md)). The shake produces a
high; the test pulls back **below the UTAD high**; the SOW develops
from this LPSY level.

A UTAD without a clean test does not produce a discrete LPSY — the
markdown begins immediately. See [[upthrust-after-distribution]] on
when the test is skipped.

### LPSY Within The Range (Distribution #2 / No-Shake)

In Distribution #2, the LPSY appears **inside the structure** as the
lower high between an interior bearish impulse and the eventual
break of the Ice. It is the **primary trade location** for the
no-shake distribution variant — see [[no-shake-phase-c]].

The book notes a labeling overlap here: an internal minor UTAD that
fails to reach the structural high can also be labeled LPSY because the
two describe the same point (a lower high inside the range that
precedes the next SOW). See [[upthrust-after-distribution]] for the
labeling-overlap discussion.

### LPSY Out Of Range — FTI + Phase E Pullbacks

Out-of-range LPSY appears as
([book p.176](../../raw/book/pages/page_176.md)):

- The **confirmation event after the Phase D breakdown** — synonymous
  with [[fall-through-the-ice|FTI]].
- **All pullbacks during Phase E** as the markdown unfolds.

### LPSY Character — Narrow Ranges, Low Volume

The book gives the diagnostic character of a good LPSY
([book p.177](../../raw/book/pages/page_177.md)):

- After breakdown of the Ice with a SOW, the upward retest should
  show **narrow price ranges** (difficulty rising).
- Preferably **low volume** (lack of buyer interest).
- A **high volume** retest is ambiguous and warrants caution — it may
  signal aggressive shorting (bearish) or latent buying pressure
  (potential failed signal).

### Discipline — Sell The LPSY, Not The SOW

Same logic as LPS: sell the corrective wave (LPSY), not the impulsive
move (SOW). Entering on the breakdown chases the move and exposes the
trader to a potential failed bearish break.

### Price Coincidence With PSY

The LPSY price sometimes coincides with the original
[[preliminary-support|Preliminary Supply (PSY)]] level
([book p.177](../../raw/book/pages/page_177.md)) — the operator
returns to the same area where the initial distribution began. Same
"price magnet" effect as the LPS-PS coincidence in accumulation.

## Why It Matters For Wyckoff Reading

- LPSY is the mirror family of LPS. Recognizing which variant
  (after-shake, within-range, out-of-range) you are looking at
  determines the structural read.
- The sell-the-LPSY / not-the-SOW discipline is the bearish version
  of the trader-discipline rule.
- LPSY = FTI in the out-of-range variant — same event, two labels.
