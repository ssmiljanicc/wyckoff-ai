# Flat Reaction

## Summary

A **flat reaction** in the archive is a shallow, low-volatility reaction that
stays in the upper part of the local range. Vol 34 uses it as a bullish clue
only if demand later proves itself through a resistance break and shallow
backup
([vol 34](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-34.html)).

## Key Points

- Vol 34 says each Bitcoin reaction had less volatility, and the third reaction
  was flat and in the top half of the range
  ([vol 34](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-34.html)).
- The report does not treat the flat reaction as an entry by itself: demand
  still has to show through a break of resistance followed by a shallow backup
  ([vol 34](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-34.html)).
- If price fails to commit above local resistance, the tactical read changes to
  watching for a retest of the low
  ([vol 34](../../../raw/crypto_archive/html/wyckoff-crypto-report-vol-34.html)).

## Why It Matters For Wyckoff Reading

- It is a crypto-archive event/modifier for low-volatility absorption inside a
  smaller range.
- It connects directly to [[action-test-confirmation]] because the flat reaction
  needs a later test or breakout.
- It should not be confused with a completed [[last-point-of-support]] unless
  the later confirmation appears.
