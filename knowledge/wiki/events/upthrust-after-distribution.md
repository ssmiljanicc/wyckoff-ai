# Upthrust After Distribution (UTAD)

## Summary

The **Upthrust After Distribution (UTAD)** is the Phase C shake in
distribution and redistribution structures
([book p.150](../../../raw/book/pages/page_150.md)). It is an upward false
break above the [[trading-range]] resistance whose purpose is to test
buyers' ability to take prices higher and capture liquidity above the
prior highs before the bearish trend begins.

The UTAD is the **mirror of the [[spring]]** in accumulation. The book
notes the UTAD is "theoretically an [[upthrust|Upthrust (UT)]]" but
when it occurs in Phase C — regardless of whether Phase B contained
earlier upthrusts — it is labeled UTAD because **the distribution
process has already happened**
([book p.150](../../../raw/book/pages/page_150.md)).

## Key Points

### What A UTAD Does

It is a **false break of resistance** that exits the
[[structures/distribution|distribution]] (or
[[structures/redistribution|redistribution]]) range
([book p.136](../../../raw/book/pages/page_136.md)). The big trader
absorbs the liquidity triggered by the false breakout:

- Breakout-buying retail and algorithmic strategies entering long on
  the new high.
- Short-position-holders capitulating into the rally.
- Smart-but-early shorts whose stops just got hit on the upward
  extension.

The volume observed is **moderate to strong**, evidencing the quantity
of crossing orders at the key zone
([book p.150](../../../raw/book/pages/page_150.md)).

### Preconditions

Same as [[spring]] ([book p.134](../../../raw/book/pages/page_134.md)):

1. Phase A has stopped the previous uptrend.
2. Phase B has built the cause (the distribution period during which
   the operator built the desired short position).

Without these, an upside probe of resistance is just a continuation of
the uptrend.

### Three Graphical Variants

Same family as the spring
([book p.137–138](../../../raw/book/pages/page_137.md)):

1. **One candle** — inverted hammer / shooting star with a long upper
   wick.
2. **Pattern of 2+ candles** — same dynamic over more time; faster
   reversal denotes greater strength.
3. **Minor structure** — a small distribution forming in the
   potential-UTAD zone that itself generates the bearish reversal.

### Minor UTAD

When the shake does not reach the structural high but takes out local
highs within the range, the event is a **minor UTAD**
([book p.151](../../../raw/book/pages/page_151.md)). The book notes:

- This denotes **greater seller control** — sellers did not even allow
  price to reach the full resistance before stepping in aggressively.
- Functionally it is still a shake at local highs and a valid Phase C
  event.
- Some Wyckoff traditions label this strictly as a
  [[last-point-of-supply|LPSY]]; the book suggests treating it as a
  minor UTAD when the structure is otherwise consistent with
  distribution.

### Terminal Upthrust & Ordinary Upthrust

The book describes two variants by intensity / location
([book p.153](../../../raw/book/pages/page_153.md)):

- **Terminal Upthrust** — like a [[spring|Terminal Shakeout]] on the
  upside. Same character as a normal UTAD but **more severe scope**:
  extremely high volume or unusually large penetration. In a short
  period of time the price re-enters the range, indicating strong
  downward pressure.
- **Ordinary Upthrust** — the upside mirror of the Ordinary Shakeout: a
  bullish shake during a **downtrend movement**, with little
  preparation. It is the typical Phase C event in
  [[structures/redistribution]] and is described as "a very interesting
  opportunity to go short since we would be trading in favor of the
  last distribution"
  ([book p.153](../../../raw/book/pages/page_153.md)).

### The UTAD Test

A test of the UTAD does not always appear
([book p.152](../../../raw/book/pages/page_152.md)). When the supply
entering at the UTAD is overwhelming, the bearish move develops
immediately as a [[sign-of-weakness]] without a clean retest.

When a test does appear, it should show **less enthusiasm than the
UTAD itself**:

- The bounce **stops below the UTAD level**.
- Price and volume ranges **reduce**.
- These together confirm buyer exhaustion → distribution scenario
  confirmed → short positions can be taken on the ceiling of the test.

A test that **does not fall below** the UTAD high, or comes in on
**higher volume**, casts doubt on the shake even if subsequent highs
are lower. Wait for additional confirmation (new shakes and successive
successful tests) before selling.

### Labeling Discipline

The book is strict
([book p.141](../../../raw/book/pages/page_141.md)):

> "A UTAD is the shaking event of the highs of the structure but it
> should also cause the bearish break and the beginning of the trend
> movement out of range. … Anything other than that would be badly
> labeled."

If the shake does not cause the eventual bearish breakout of the
structure, it is by definition not a UTAD — it is a Phase B test (a
[[st-as-msos|UA / mSOS]] or just an "ST in B"). See
[[concepts/labeling-is-last-step]].

### Diagnostics That Favor A UTAD Over A Real Break

Two clues raise the probability that an upside probe is a UTAD (not a
real bullish break)
([book p.141–142](../../../raw/book/pages/page_141.md)):

1. **Phase B contained a mSOW** — the lower-end test denoted seller
   strength, so the upper-end probe is more likely a fake-out (see
   [[st-as-msow]]).
2. **Price re-enters the range quickly after the break.** Failure to
   stay above denotes weakness and adds probability that the break was
   false.

## Why It Matters For Wyckoff Reading

- The UTAD is **the** position-opening event of distribution. Best
  risk/reward in the structure: stop just above the UTAD high, target
  the [[concepts/creek-and-ice|ice]] and beyond.
- Differentiating UTAD vs Terminal Upthrust vs Ordinary Upthrust
  matters tactically — they sit in different structures (distribution,
  high-volume distribution, redistribution).
- The UTAD test discipline matters: a clean test below the UTAD level
  is the entry, not the UTAD itself.
