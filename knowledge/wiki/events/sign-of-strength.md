# Sign of Strength (SOS) and Major SOS

## Summary

The **Sign of Strength (SOS)** is the bullish breakout event (event #6
of the methodology) in an accumulation structure. It is the upward move
that originates from the low of Phase C — either the [[spring]] or the
[[last-point-of-support|LPS]] — and produces the break of the upper
range boundary (the [[concepts/creek-and-ice|Creek]])
([book p.160](../../../raw/book/pages/page_160.md)).

The SOS produces the **second Change of Character (CHoCh)** of the
structure: the market exits the range and enters a trending phase
([book p.155](../../../raw/book/pages/page_155.md)). The first CHoCh was
the [[automatic-rally]] at the start of Phase A.

A SOS that successfully breaks the structure and produces a markup
trend is often called a **Major SOS** to distinguish it from the
**minor SOS** described below. The book uses both labels.

The distribution mirror is [[sign-of-weakness]].

## Key Points

### Where The SOS Originates

The SOS originates in the **low of Phase C**
([book p.160](../../../raw/book/pages/page_160.md)):

- In **Accumulation #1** (with shake) — from the [[spring]] low.
- In **Accumulation #2** (no shake) — from the
  [[last-point-of-support|LPS]] inside the range.

The trader is positioned long from the Phase C entry. The SOS is the
move that validates the trade.

### Appearance — Wide Ranges, Increased Volume

The SOS is "an environment of speed"
([book p.155](../../../raw/book/pages/page_155.md)). The move develops
through candles that show:

- Relative **increase in price ranges** (wide trend bars).
- **Increase in volume** traded.
- Fluid breaks of previous liquidity levels (previous highs, the minor
  Creek).

This is the visual signature of market imbalance — the aggressive
buyers have control, and the absorption that took place during Phase B
is paying off.

### The Breakout Without Volume Scenario

A volume increase is the **typical** signal but not strictly required
([book p.156–157](../../../raw/book/pages/page_156.md)). When the
remaining floating supply is **already very low** (Phase B absorption
was effective), the operator does not need to push volume — buyers
encounter little supply and the break happens easily. Diagnostic:

- Narrow-range candles + middling volume + clean break of resistance →
  low-supply environment, not a fake break.
- This is rarer than the wide-range version but structurally equivalent.

### Diagnostic Cues That The Break Is Genuine

Four cues, in approximate order of importance
([book p.158–159](../../../raw/book/pages/page_158.md)):

1. **Price does not immediately re-enter the range.** The most
   reliable signal. If price holds above the broken Creek, demand is
   real.
2. **Subsequent candles look "unintentional"** — narrow range,
   intertwined, low volume — consistent with a corrective pullback
   rather than a reversal.
3. **Distance of the rupture.** No fixed rule, but the breakout that
   moves an obvious distance away from the structure adds confidence.
4. **Wide range bars with elevated volume on the break itself** —
   the textbook image.

If price re-enters the range after the break, the event is **not** a
SOS — it is an [[upthrust|Upthrust (UT)]] — see
[[failed-signal]].

### Quality Of A Major SOS

For the SOS to qualify as a **Major SOS**
([book p.161](../../../raw/book/pages/page_161.md)):

- The bullish movement shows **ease of movement** through previous
  levels.
- It reaches **at least the midpoint of the range**.
- Any subsequent regression remains **above the spring low** (or
  above the LPS low in Accumulation #2).

Failing these conditions, the move is a **minor SOS (mSOS)**
([book p.162](../../../raw/book/pages/page_162.md)) — strength but not yet
range-breaking. mSOS can also describe a strength move inside Phase B
that has SOS characteristics but does not break the structure.

### SOS Bar — The Entry Trigger

The **SOS Bar** is a specific bar pattern used as an entry trigger
([book p.162–163](../../../raw/book/pages/page_162.md)):

- Wide bullish bar.
- Close **at the highs** of the range.
- **Increased volume**.
- Alternatively, a **bullish gap** with same intent.

It signals strong demand of quality — the institutional buying
moment. When a SOS bar appears in the operative zone (after a shake,
after a break, or in trend), it is the definitive sign that
professionals are supporting the upward move and provides a clean entry
trigger for longs.

### Breakout Is Not An Entry Opportunity

The book is explicit
([book p.159](../../../raw/book/pages/page_159.md)): the break itself
should **not** be traded:

- The break zone has too many crossing orders; market control can flip.
- A potential "SOS" can turn into an Upthrust without warning.
- The correct entry is the **confirmation test** (LPS / BUEC) — see
  [[last-point-of-support]], [[back-up-to-the-edge-of-the-creek]].

## Why It Matters For Wyckoff Reading

- The SOS is the **trigger** that confirms the entire accumulation
  thesis. Until it prints, every Phase B / Phase C label is provisional.
- Major SOS vs minor SOS is the **structural-vs-intra-range** distinction
  — only a Major SOS validates the accumulation thesis at the
  structure level.
- The discipline "do not enter on the break, wait for the confirmation
  test" is the difference between catching the move and getting
  shaken out by an Upthrust.
