# No-Shake Phase C (Accumulation #2 / Distribution #2)

## Summary

In the **No-Shake** variant of Phase C, the structure does not produce
a terminal [[spring]] (or [[upthrust-after-distribution|UTAD]]) before
the trend move begins. Instead, the operational opportunity moves to
the **Phase D test of the broken range boundary** — the
[[last-point-of-support|LPS]] / [[back-up-to-the-edge-of-the-creek|BUEC]]
in accumulation, or [[last-point-of-supply|LPSY]] /
[[fall-through-the-ice|FTI]] in distribution.

The book is explicit on both sides: the shake "is the central handling
manoeuvre but **does not always occur**"
([book p.74](../../../raw/book/pages/page_074.md);
[book p.82](../../../raw/book/pages/page_082.md)). The two no-shake
schematics are formally named **Basic Accumulation #2** and **Basic
Distribution #2** in the methodology
([book p.40–45](../../../raw/book/pages/page_040.md)).

## Key Points

### Why The Shake Doesn't Happen

The shake's structural job is to grab the final liquidity above /
below the structure. When the absorption during Phase B has been
**thorough enough that no significant liquidity remains** at the
range extremes, the operator does not need to take price there
([book p.75](../../../raw/book/pages/page_075.md);
[book p.83](../../../raw/book/pages/page_083.md)).

Functionally: the path of least resistance has been verified inside
the range; no further test outside is required.

This typically happens when:

- Phase B was unusually long and the strong-hand / weak-hand mix is
  already favorable (see [[book-chapter-11|book-chapter-11]]).
- Multiple in-range tests have already absorbed available stock.

### A Smaller Shake May Still Occur

Chapter 23 notes that the Phase C shake may not necessarily **sweep the
extremes of the structure**
([book p.184–185](../../../raw/book/pages/page_184.md)). When the shake
stays inside the structure, it can be labeled:

- **Minor spring** / **minor UTAD** — internal shake that still produces
  the reversal.
- **In function of LPS / LPSY** — the same action labeled by its
  Phase D role.

The book's pragmatic note is consistent with
[[concepts/labeling-is-last-step]]: the labels are not what matters;
the function is. If absorption is complete, the trend move begins from
inside the range and the entry shifts to LPS / LPSY.

### Operational Implication — LPS / LPSY Is The Primary Trade

In the no-shake schematics, the **primary trade location is the
Phase D confirmation event**
([book p.41, 45](../../../raw/book/pages/page_041.md)):

- **Accumulation #2** — entry at the [[last-point-of-support|LPS]] /
  [[back-up-to-the-edge-of-the-creek|BUEC]] after the upward break of
  the [[concepts/creek-and-ice|creek]].
- **Distribution #2** — entry at the [[last-point-of-supply|LPSY]] /
  [[fall-through-the-ice|FTI]] after the downward break of the
  [[concepts/creek-and-ice|ice]].

The R:R is somewhat worse than a Spring #3-style direct entry, but the
structural validity is identical — both schematics produce the same
markup / markdown.

### Diagnostic Cues That No Shake Will Happen

These are inferences, not guarantees, but they raise probability:

- **Extended Phase B** with decreasing volume — late-range action
  showing absorption is exhausting available supply / demand.
- **Repeated narrow-range, low-volume tests** of both ends of the
  range with no follow-through — see [[secondary-test]] discussion of
  Phase B tests.
- **No clear Phase B UA / mSOW** that would foreshadow a Phase C
  shake (see [[st-as-msos]], [[st-as-msow]]).

When Phase D begins with a clean upward / downward break and the
expected shake has not appeared, the no-shake schematic is the read.

## Why It Matters For Wyckoff Reading

- The no-shake variant is **equally valid** — the methodology is not
  invalidated when a spring does not appear; the primary trade simply
  moves.
- A trader committed to a spring-only entry rule will miss
  Accumulation #2 setups entirely.
- The label for an internal-shake-or-LPS event is less important than
  recognizing that absorption is complete and the markup is starting.
